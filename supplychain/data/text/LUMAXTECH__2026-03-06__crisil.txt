Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has upgraded its rating on the long-term bank facilities of
Lumax Auto Technologies Ltd (LATL; part of the
Lumax group
)
to
‘Crisil AA/Stable’
from ‘Crisil AA-/Positive’
and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the short-term bank facilities
and
commercial paper programme of the company.
The rating action reflects the better-than-anticipated improvement in the operating performance of the
Lumax group
during fiscal 2026, as reflected in the strong ~38% growth in revenu
e
to Rs 3,453 crore
for the first nine months of fiscal 2026 with operating margin sustained at a healthy level of 13.2% (
13.1% in fiscal 2025)
. This follows revenue growth of 29% in fiscal 2025 and 53% in fiscal 2024, driven by healthy growth in standalone performance, strong contribution of acquired entities, and the ramp-up of operations of various subsidiaries and joint ventures (JVs). This momentum is expected to sustain over the medium term, supported by established relationship with key customers, di
versified product profile and clientele across most segments, and a conscious shift towards electric vehicle (EV) agnostic and alternative fuel products. The steady operating profitability, combined with rising scale of operations, has resulted in high annual cash generation. Efficient working capital management and limited reliance on debt have strengthened the financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to cyclicality in the domestic automotive (auto) industry and high e
xposure to a few original equipment manufacturers (OEMs).
The strong performance of the Lumax group in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 was driven by high growth of subsidiary
IAC International Automotive India Pvt Ltd
(IAC India) riding on healthy offtake by key OEMs, especially in the passenger vehicle (PV) segment. Strong growth in the two- and three-wheeler segment, and scaling up of other JVs and subsidiaries, especially in areas of safety, emission and comfort requirements in automobiles, also su
pported the growth.
Acquisition of
controlling interest in Greenfuel Energy Solutions Pvt Ltd (Greenfuel) for its alternative fuels business has helped the Lumax group enter the green and alternative fuels segment (across compressed natural gas [CNG], hydrogen and other applications), which is expected to witness strong and accelerated growth in the coming years
.
Consolidated revenue is likely to grow 15-20% over the medium term, driven by increasing contribution of the recently acquired Greenfuel, suste
nance of healthy growth at subsidiaries and JVs with monetisation of new orders, contribution from existing products such as lighting, automatic gear shifter and sheet metal, and incremental revenue from new products such as alternative fuels, mechatronics, besides strong aftermarket demand. The business risk profile will be supported by diversity in terms of segmental and product profiles and established customer relations.
Healthy growth in the aftermarket segment, growth in new subsidiaries (Greenfuel),
strong growth in the mechatronics category, and steady growth of IAC India should help improve operating margin by 100-150 basis points over the medium term.
As on September 30, 2025, consolidated debt (including leases) for LATL stood at Rs 1,111 crore (Rs 902 crore as on March 31, 2025) with adjusted networth improving to around Rs 1,270 crore from Rs 1,273 crore as on March 31, 2025. Despite debt availed to partly fund the recent acquisition of balance stake in IAC India, overall debt is likely to remai
n at Rs 850-1,000 crore over the next 2-3 years, factoring in debt servicing, while moderate annual capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 200-250 crore will be funded through annual accrual of over Rs 350 crore. Adjusted gearing is likely to stabilise below 0.7 time over the medium term, while interest coverage ratio will remain above 7 times. Consolidated unencumbered cash surplus of ~Rs 400 crore as on September 30, 2025, supports liquidity. While the Lumax group will remain open to growth by inorganic route,
the size of such expansion and means of funding will be monitorable.
Analytical Approach
Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of LATL and its subsidiaries and JVs. The companies, collectively referred to as the Lumax group, are in the same business, under a common management, and have significant operational and financial synergies. Goodwill arising from the acquisition of stake in IAC India and Greenfuel has been amortised over five years. Other intangibles arising from
the acquisitions have been amortised over 10 years.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Established market position and strong relationships with key customers
LATL has a strong market position in the auto lighting products industry and healthy relationship with key customers, Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd(M&M; ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Bajaj Auto Ltd (BAL; ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Maruti Suzuki India Ltd (MSIL; ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and Honda Motors & Scooters India. The Lumax group supplies two- and three-wheeler lighting products and two
-wheeler chassis to BAL, and four-wheeler gear shift assemblies to MSIL, Toyota Motor Corporation, Honda Motor Company, and Renault-Nissan. To diversify its product offerings, the group is supplying swing arms for two-wheelers and trailing arms for three-wheelers. A network of over 400 distributors across India for aftermarket sales strengthens the business risk profile. Moreover, the group continues to pursue JVs to augment product profiles and expand geographically. Additionally, the acquisition of IAC In
dia and Greenfuel has enhanced the product portfolio of LATL, enabling supply of integrated solutions to its clientele, thereby increasing the content supplied per vehicle.
The dependence on the two-wheeler segment reduced from 43% in fiscal 2022 to 22% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026.
Steady operating profitability
Polypropylene (PP), the main raw material used for making plastic powder, is a downstream petrochemical product. Hence, the price of PP is directly linked to crude oil rates, which are
highly volatile. Also, steel prices have been volatile in the past 4-5 years.
As most customers are OEMs, the Lumax group does not have the cushion to fully pass on increase in input rates as prices are revised annually. However, due to benefits of scale and improving product mix, the group has been able to stabilise its operating profitability at a healthy 14-15% in recent years. The management aims to improve operating profitability to ~20% in the long term by enhancing the share of value-added products i
n the sales mix and through economies of scale.
Healthy financial risk profile
The financial risk profile has remained healthy despite the debt-funded acquisitions, which led to higher borrowings (Rs 1,111 crore, including lease liabilities, as on September 30, 2025, compared with ~Rs 902 crore as on March 31, 2025). Still, debt protection metrics remain stable, driven by steady profitability, with interest coverage expected over 6 times for fiscal 2026 as against 6.23 times for fiscal 2025. The ratio of n
et debt to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) is expected to improve to 1.2-1.3 times from 1.35 times. With progressive debt repayment, moderate capex needs and efficient working capital management, debt is expected to remain at Rs 800-1,000 crore, supported by healthy annual accrual of over Rs 350 crore. The interest coverage is expected to rise to over 7 times and gearing to settle under 0.7 time over the medium term. The ratio of net debt to Ebitda is expected to improv
e steadily as well.
LATL’s management has announced its intent to increase revenue to Rs 11,000 crore by fiscal 2031 through a mix of organic and inorganic growth. The company has made a few acquisitions in the recent past, which have helped scale up revenue and cash flow. Sizeable debt funding of large acquisitions in the future and the impact on the financial risk profile will bear watching.
Key Rating Drivers -

knesses
Susceptibility to cyclicality in the domestic automotive industry
LATL supplie
s automotive components to OEMs in the domestic market, which accounts for a sizeable portion of its revenue. The aftermarket accounts for 11-15% of the revenue. Exports, which typically enjoy higher margin, remain minimal. This exposes the company’s topline to demand slowdown in the domestic automotive market and intense pricing pressure in the competitive aftermarket. That said, with new business wins from key OEMs and with ramp-up of operations at subsidiaries and JVs, LATL has outperformed the overall a
utomotive components sector in terms of revenue growth.
On a steady state basis, given its high dependence on the domestic market, LATL’s ability to continuously enhance the share of business from existing OEMs and add customers, as well as enhance its product basket, will be critical to drive sustained revenue growth and derisk its operations from the cyclical nature of the domestic automotive demand.
Exposure to customer concentration risk
Acquisition of IAC India has enhanced the revenue profile of t
he Lumax group, with a higher contribution from M&M reducing reliance on BAL. However, these two customers still contribute 40-45% to the group’s revenue. Despite strong customer relations, revenue and profitability are vulnerable to any change in the business plans of the key clients. As a strategic partner to M&M and BAL, the Lumax group is prone to fluctuations in demand on an ongoing basis. This may lead to volatility in revenue and margin, especially when capacity is underutilised.
Liquidity
Strong
Consolidated unencumbered cash surplus of ~Rs 400 crore as on September 30, 2025, along with healthy annual accrual of over Rs 350 crore, supports overall liquidity. Working capital limit of ~Rs 350 crore (on a standalone basis), was utilised extensively, at over 90% over the six months through December 2025. The group has annual long-term debt obligation of Rs ~150 crore starting fiscal 2026 and plans capex of Rs 200-250 crore per annum. Cash accrual should suffice to fund the debt obligation and capex, w
hile incremental working capital needs will be met through bank debt.
Outlook
Stable
Crisil Ratings believes the revenue of LATL will continue to grow in healthy double digits over the medium term, supported by the company’s diversified product mix, established clientele and increasing contribution from subsidiaries. The company is also likely to maintain steady operating profitability. Healthy cash accrual and progressive debt repayment will help improve debt metrics over the medium term.

About the Company
The Lumax group is part of the DK Jain group of companies.
LATL was incorporated in 1981 as Lumax Auto Electricals Pvt Ltd, and was renamed Dhanesh Auto Electricals Pvt Ltd in 1988 and as Dhanesh Auto Electricals Ltd in 1998. The company got its current name in 2006. LATL has divisions catering to lighting systems; and sheet metal components, gear shifters and moulded parts. Lighting products (head lamps, tail lamps, and blinkers) are manufactured in Pune, sheet metal components (mainly
chassis for BAL's two-wheelers) in Aurangabad, and moulded parts (for Honda Motorcycle & Scooter India Pvt Ltd) in Bengaluru. The company's aftermarket division (domestic and exports) trades in automotive components such as lightings, accessories, and audio and navigation systems.
Lumax DK Auto Industries Ltd (LDK), incorporated in 1997, was a wholly owned subsidiary of LATL and was merged with the latter in December 2018. LDK manufactured auto components, including gear shifter assemblies, head and tail l
amp assemblies, moulded parts, and parking brakes, with the bulk of revenue coming from the supply of lighting products and moulded parts to BAL, and the remaining from gear shifter assemblies and parking brakes for MSIL. The manufacturing plants are in Pantnagar (Uttarakhand) and Manesar (Haryana).
The gear shifter business was demerged, effective April 2014, into Lumax Mannoh Allied Technologies Ltd (LMAT),
a 55% subsidiary which manufactures automated manual transmission (AMT) and automatic gear shifte
r systems, head and tail lamp assemblies, moulded parts, and parking brakes.
LMAT
exports automatic gear shifters and is working with the JV partner, Mannoh Industrial Co Ltd, Japan
­
­
, to increase reach to new markets.
IAC India
is a Tier 1 interior systems and components supplier to key automotive OEMs, including M&M, MSIL, Volkswagen India and Volvo Eicher Commercial Vehicles. IAC India recently become a 100% subsidiary after LATL acquired the India business of IAC through LIVE effective March 2023.
Lu
max Cornaglia Auto Technologies Pvt Ltd (LCAT) is a 50:50 JV between LATL and Officine Metallurgiche G Cornaglia, SpA, Italy, through the Italian company's subsidiary, Cornaglia Metallurgical Products India Pvt Ltd. The JV commenced operations in fiscal 2008 and manufactures and supplies air-intake systems and
urea tanks. It commands 100% share of business with Volkswagen and Tata Motors
. The manufacturing facilities are in Pune.
Lumax Management Services Pvt Ltd (LMSPL), 100% subsidiary of LATL provides c
orporate support services to the DK Jain group of companies. The company provides services such as SAP-ERP support, IT/ITES, skill development and human resource support services to LATL and LIL.
Lumax FAE Technologies Pvt Ltd was established in July 2017 by LATL and FAE to manufacture oxygen sensors for the Indian auto industry, with LATL owning 84% and FAE, 16%. The facility at Manesar will have capacity to manufacture 2.5 million oxygen sensors.
Lumax Ituran Telematics Pvt Ltd is a 50:50 JV between LA
TL and Ituran, Israel. It was formed in fiscal 2017, to produce telematics products and
has successfully commenced supply of telematics parts to Daimler India in fiscal 2024.
Set up in September 2021, Lumax Alps Alpine India Pvt Ltd (LAAIPL) is a 50% subsidiary of LATL, with the balance stake held by Alps Alpine Corporation Ltd, Japan. LAAIPL will manufacture and sell electric devices and components, including software related to the auto industry.
Lumax Yokowo Technologies Pvt Ltd (LYTPL) is a subsidiary
of LATL established in February 2020. LATL holds 50% while the rest is held by Yokowo Company, Japan. LYTPL manufactures antennas and other communication products.
Lumax JOPP Allied Technologies Pvt Ltd (LJATPL) was set up in April 2019, in collaboration with JOPP, Germany, to manufacture transmission products. LJATPL holds 50% in LJOPP and JOPP holds the remaining.
Lumax Ancilliary Ltd was incorporated in 1982 and is a 100% subsidiary of LATL. The Delhi-based company is a leading manufacturer of wire ha
rness signaling equipment and control cables, and supplies to major OEMs such as MSIL, Honda, Tata Motors, BAL and M&M. The manufacturing facilities are in Bhiwadi (Rajasthan), Chakan (Maharashtra) and Rudrapur (Uttarakhand).
Lumax Auto Technologies has acquired 60% stake in the alternative fuels business of Greenfuel to enter the growing green and alternative fuels market, particularly for CNG and hydrogen systems. Major customers are MSIL and Tata.
