Detailed Rationale
CRISIL Ratings has reaffirmed its 'CRISIL A+/Stable/CRISIL A1' ratings on the bank
loan
facilities and non-convertible debentures (NCDs) for Hero Motors Ltd (HML).
The reaffirmation factors in the healthy business risk profile of HML, driven by improving contribution of high value-added components leading to higher margins as well as increasing revenue diversification over the years. However, o
perating performance is expected to be subdued for fiscal 2024 with consolidated revenue estimated at Rs 1,100-1,200 crore with a lower-than-expected operating margin of 10-11%. This is mainly due to a
slowdown in the exports segment impacting revenue and profitability. The subsidiaries, which were loss making in fiscal 2023, are expected to contribute positively to the overall earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) in fiscal 2024, driven by significant ramp up in Hewland Engineering Ltd. Other subsidiaries are also expected to scale up in the near term and will remain monitorable. Operating performance may recover going forward with expected revival in demand and launch
of new products; this will remain monitorable. Increased revenue contribution from the higher-margin segments should lead to gradual margin expansion over the medium term.
The reaffirmation also factors in the strong financial risk profile with consolidated debt expected at about Rs 300 crore as on March 31, 2024. To fund growth, the company is expected to undertake capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 500-600 crore in the next 3-4 fiscals. However, given healthy cash accrual, existing liquidity and fund-rai
sing plans, there should not be significant, debt-funded capex. Hence, net debt/Ebitda should remain comfortable below 3 times over the medium term.
The ratings continue to reflect the healthy business
risk profile of HML, driven by improving revenue diversification and strong financial risk profile despite the sizeable capex. These strengths are partially offset by investment towards subsidiaries in the ramp-up phase, customer concentration in the revenue profile and exposure to cyclicality in the auto
motive (auto) components industry.
Analytical Approach
CRISIL Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of HML, its subsidiaries and joint ventures. This is because all these entities have common management and strong business and financial linkages.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description
Strengths
:
Healthy business risk profile, driven by increasing
revenue diversification
:
HML has gradually added higher value-added components and built capabilities in more complex parts, which has led to significant improvement in product diversity and operating performance in recent years. Contribution from the low-margin alloy and metals business reduced to about 55% in fiscal 2023 from nearly 90% in fiscal 2018.
Geographic diversity has improved, with export contributing to over 40% of estimated revenue in fiscal 2023, compared with minimal share in fiscal 2018. Revenue rose significantly to around Rs 1,100 crore in fiscal 2023 from Rs 703 in fiscal 2021.
While revenue growth has been subdued in fiscal 2024, recovery is expected over the medium term driven by product addition and ramp up in subsidiaries. Furthermore, the company expects contribution from the electric vehicles business to increase significantly over the medium term. The business risk profile may remain healthy, driven by growth in the higher value-added segments and addition of customers and products, leading to improving revenue diversity.
Strong financial risk profile despite high capex intensity
:
Consolidated debt is projected at Rs 310 crore and networth at about Rs 385 crore as on March 31, 2024. The company is expected to undertake significant capex of Rs 500-600 crore in the next 3-4 fiscals. However, owing to healthy cash accrual and fund-raising plans, the capex will be largely funded through internal cash accrual.
Despite subdued operating profitability in fiscal 2024, debt protection metrics remain healthy, as reflected in estimated interest coverage ratio of over 4 times in the near term. These are expected to improve going forward with no significant, debt-funded capex.
Weaknesses
:
Investment towards subsidiaries in ramp up phase
:
The
company
has
acquired stake in Hewland Engineering Ltd to gain capabilities in the high-performance two-wheeler and four-wheeler segment. Similarly, it has set up other subsidiaries recently,
such as HYM Systems Pvt Ltd and Hero EDU Systems Pvt Ltd
for expansion into new geographies and product lines. HML also acquired 100% stake in Spur in November 2023 from Hero Cycles Ltd to cater to existing customer of HML and to new customers. Until fiscal 2023,
the subsidiaries had negligible contribution to the o
perating performance of the company. While Hewland Engineering Ltd is expected to be profitable in fiscal 2024, other subsidiaries are yet to contribute to overall operating profitability.
Hence, these subsidiaries will require financial support
for funding their capex in the near term. Ramp up in operations of the subsidiaries and their contribution to the overall operating performance over the medium term will be monitorable.
Customer concentration in revenue and exposure to cyclicality in the auto components industry
:
Revenue is largely dependent on demand from original equipment manufacturers in the two-wheeler industry, which in turn depends on several factors, such as disposable incomes, consumer preferences, credit availability and fuel prices. Furthermore, while the company has gradually added new customers, the top three customers contributed to over 55% of revenue in fiscal 2023. This dependence poses a risk to operating performance in case of any significant slowdown.
Liquidity
: Strong
Cash and liquid investments are estimated at Rs 87 crore as on December 31, 2023. Fund-based limit was utilised more than 90% during the 12 months through January 2023. Healthy cash accrual, expected at Rs 80-100 crore per annum in fiscals 2024 and 2025, will sufficiently cover moderate debt obligation and working capital requirement. The company has significant capex of Rs 500-600 crore planned over the next 3-4 fiscals, to be funded via internal cash accrual.
Outlook
:
Stable
The business risk profile of HML will remain healthy over the medium term, with addition of customers, products and ramp up of subsidiaries. Financial risk profile may continue to be strong due to healthy cash accrual, fund-raising plans and no significant, debt-funded capex.
Rating Sensitivity Factors
Up
ward factors:
Continued high revenue growth and operating margin sustaining over 14%
Addition of customers and products, leading to revenue diversification and significant improvement in cash accrual
Sustained improvement in cash accruals or any equity infusion
Down
ward factors:
Lower-than-expected revenue growth or delayed ramp up in subsidiaries with operating margin sustaining below 10%
Stretch in working capital cycle
Large, debt-funded capex or acquisition
About the Company
Incorporated in 1998, HML is a part of the OP Munjal group, whose flagship company is Hero Cycles Ltd (‘
CRISIL A+/Negative/CRISIL A1’)
. HML manufactures and supplies auto components including gearboxes, continuous variable transmission and other components for the two-wheeler and electric bicycles segments.
The company had demerged its auto businesses into Hero Cycles Ltd with effect from April 1, 2014.
The auto components division of Hero Cycles Ltd was demerged back into HML in December 2022 with effect
from
April 1, 2022
.
