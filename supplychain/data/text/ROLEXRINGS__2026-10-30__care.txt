Rationale and key rating drivers
The upward revision in the ratings assigned to the bank facilities of Rolex Rings Limited (RRL) is on the back of successful
resolution of impending Right to Recompensate (ROR) liability while maintaining strong financial risk profile and liquidity. The
ratings also factor in sustained healthy scale of operations in FY26 (refers to the period April 1 to March 31) despite geopolitical
uncertainty along with healthy profitability.
The ratings continue to derive strength from the experience of promoters in the auto component industry, established track record
of operations in manufacturing of bearing rings along with its status as an approved supplier for reputed clientele.
The ratings, however, continue to remain constrained on account of high customer concentration, susceptibility of profitability to
volatile raw material prices as well as foreign exchange rate and its presence in a highly competitive industry with demand linked
to cyclical end-user industries (including automobile, engineering and capital goods and heavy machinery).
CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has withdrawn the ratings assigned to the fund based & non-fund based bank facilities
of Union Bank of India and Punjab National Bank, fund based facilities of Indian Overseas Bank and Bank of India on the basis of
‘No Due Certificate’ with immediate effect, as the company has repaid the aforementioned facility in full and there is no amount
outstanding under the said facilities as on date.

Detailed description of key rating drivers
Key strengths
Successful resolution of ROR liability while maintaining strong liquidity
During FY25, the company had received a demand notice aggregating ₹227 crore from its erstwhile consortium of banks, including
compounded interest on the original RoR sacrifice amount. Following a mutually agreed independent legal review, the liability was
settled at ₹101 crore in Q4FY26. Consequently, RRL recognised an additional provision of ₹50.4 crore in FY26, over and above
the ₹50.6 crore provided earlier, and fully discharged the obligation on March 31, 2026, resulting in the extinguishment of the
RoR liability. Despite the resultant cash outflow, the company's liquidity profile remained strong, supported by free cash and bank
balances of ₹357.6 crore as on March 31, 2026, negligible utilisation of working capital limits, and the absence of long-term debt.

Healthy scale of operations and profitability
Despite continued global inflationary pressures and moderation in demand from the US market, RRL’s total operating income
(TOI) remained resilient at ₹1,181.28 crore in FY26 vis-à-vis ₹1,167.89 crore in FY25. The moderation in demand from the US
was largely offset by higher offtake from European market, supported by addition of new plant locations of key customers, along
with sustained domestic demand. Consequently, exports accounted for 44% of total sales in FY26 as against 52% in FY25, while
the share of domestic sales increased to 56% from 48% during the same period.
RRL’s profitability remained robust, with the PBILDT margin improving to 22.69% in FY26 from 21.72% in FY25, supported by its
focus on manufacturing high-precision engineered components and favourable product mix.
Further, as per the provisional financial results for Q1FY27, the company reported TOI of ₹304 crore as against ₹292 crore in
Q1FY26, while the PBILDT margin stood at 22.70% compared with 22.23% in the corresponding period of the previous year.
Going forward, CareEdge Ratings expects RRL’s operational performance to sustain supported by its established relationships with
marquee global customers, diversified presence across geographies and user industries, and continued focus on high-precision
engineering products.
Strong financial risk profile
RRL’s financial risk profile continued to remain strong with overall gearing of 0.01x as on March 31, 2026 with no exposure to
long term debt and strong net worth base of ₹1212.85 crore.
With negligible reliance on debt, debt coverage indicators remained strong in FY26 marked by PBILDT interest coverage of 186
times (PY: 107 times) and total debt/ PBILDT of 0.05x (P.Y.: 0.17x)
CareEdge Ratings expects RRL to sustain its strong financial risk profile on the back of no major debt funded capex planned over
the medium term. Furthermore, as indicated by the management, the company is also in the process of surrendering its existing
fund based facilities with envisaged healthy cashflow from operations.
Experienced promoters with established track record of operations
The promoters of RRL, i.e., the members of the Madeka family, are well-qualified and have vast experience in the auto components
industry which is evident from the satisfactory operations of over four decades. RRL is into operations since 1980 and is one of
the leading suppliers of forged bearing rings and various automobile components like engine, transmission, chassis, and exhaust
system parts depicting established track record of operations in auto components industry.
Approved supplier for reputed clientele
RRL caters to the requirement of engineered capital goods & automobile sector and has a reputed clientele comprising of leading
bearing and auto component manufacturers. Due to its long-standing relationship with the customers as an approved vendor for
various parts, RRL is able to secure repeat orders from its customers. The company derives around 44% (PY:52%) of its revenue
from exports as most of its customers are global players with presence across various countries primarily, USA and Europe. The
moderation in exports in FY26 is on account of reduced exports to USA on account of increase in tariff on Indian exports to USA.
Key weaknesses
Customer concentration risk
RRL is exposed to customer concentration risk with top customer groups accounting for ~77% of its total sales in FY26 (~84% in
FY25). However, RRL has long-standing relationships with majority of its customers for over a decade. RRL, being a Tier II
manufacturer in auto component supply chain, supplies bearing rings and other components to Tier I manufacturers who in turn
supply components to original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) primarily from auto industry which limits its bargaining power
with its customers who are relatively large players in the industry.
Vulnerability of profit margins to adverse fluctuation in raw material prices and foreign exchange rates
Steel and its alloys form the key raw material required for manufacturing of bearing rings and auto components. The prices of
steel and its alloys, being commodity items, are volatile in nature. Further, RRL does not have any long -term supply agreements
with steel suppliers. Consequently, RRL’s profitability is exposed to adverse movement in raw material prices. However, the sales
contracts with most of RRL’s clients are revised on a quarterly basis, which mitigates its exposure to any adverse fluctuations in
raw material prices to a certain extent. Also, due to its exports, which contributed around 44% of its TOI (PY: 52%) in FY26, RRL
is exposed to forex rates fluctuation risk. However, RRL has a partial natural hedge by way of imports for its export receivables,
while the balance is hedged largely by way of use of working capital borrowings in foreign currency as well as forward contracts.
RRL imported ~12% of its total raw material consumed during FY26 (15% in FY25) and reported foreign exchange gain of ₹37.78
crore (PY: ₹13.09 crore) during FY26.

Presence in a competitive industry with demand linked to the cyclicality in various end-user industries; albeit
stable demand outlook
RRL operates in the highly competitive auto component industry and engineered & capital good segment, with demand largely
linked to end-user segments such as automobiles, engineering, capital goods, and railways. The global demand outlook for
engineered and capital goods is expected to grow moderately in CY26, supported by investments in infrastructure, energy
transition, industrial automation, and manufacturing expansion. Growth will be strongest in Asia, while North America benefits
from reshoring and supply-chain diversification initiatives. Further, the India auto component industry is expected to grow in CY26,
supported by strong domestic demand, higher OEM offtake, and a resilient aftermarket segment. While exports remain subject to
global macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainties, the medium-term outlook is favourable, driven by growth in domestic vehicle
production, infrastructure spending, localisation initiatives, OEM capacity expansions, and continued policy support for
manufacturing.
Liquidity: Strong
RRL’s liquidity remained strong marked by nil working capital utilization, nil scheduled debt repayments, healthy cash flow from
operations and sufficient liquidity in the form of free cash and bank balance. The company has maintained free cash and bank
balance (including liquid investments) of ₹357.59 crore as on March 31, 2026 (₹309.85 crore as on March 31,2025).
The average fund-based utilization of working capital limits for the past 12 months ended March 2026 remained nil which is
facilitated by healthy cash flow from operations of ₹ 149.31 crore in FY26 (PY: ₹ 242.86 crore).
RRL’s operations are working capital intensive on account of large inventory holding due to a large product range and credit period
required to be offered to its established client base. However, with efficient management of receivables and inventory days, RRL’s
operating cycle remained stable at 133 days in FY26 (PY: 129 days).

Rajkot based (Gujarat), RRL (Erstwhile; Rolex Rings Private Limited) was established as a partnership firm by Mr. Rupesh D.
Madeka in 1980 and later on reconstituted as a public limited company in 2021 and is presently managed by Mr. Manesh D.
Madeka and family. RRL is engaged in manufacturing (forging and machining) of bearing rings and auto -components like engine
parts, transmission parts, exhaust system parts, chassis parts, etc. It caters to the requirement of reputed clientele comprising
leading bearing manufacturers as well as global and domestic auto -component manufacturers. The company has two
manufacturing units which are located near Rajkot in Gujarat. RRL’s debt was restructured under Corporate Debt Restructuring
(CDR) mechanism in FY13, and the company closed CDR loans in FY22. In August 2021, RRL got listed on National Stock Exchange
(NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) and raised ₹.731 crore through Initial Public Offer (IPO). Out of this, ~₹.56 crore was
infused in the company as equity, while balance was offer for sale and paid to stake selling shareholder ‘Rivendell PE LLC”.
