Detailed rationale
Crisil Ratings has upgraded its rating on the long-term bank facilities of Sharda Motor Industries Limited (SMIL) to
‘Crisil AA/Stable’
from ‘Crisil AA-/Positive’ and has reaffirmed the short-term rating at  ‘Crisil A1+’.
The upgrade reflects an improvement in the business risk profile, marked by growth in revenue to
Rs 3,398 crore in fiscal 2026 (Rs 2,839 crore in fiscal 2025). Growth was driven by higher volume in the exhaust systems segment across passenger vehicles (PVs) and commercial vehicles (CVs), along with improved realisations of precious metals such as platinum and palladium used in catalytic converters. Further, the company reported revenue of Rs 1,011 crore in the first quarter of fiscal year 2027, aided by capacity expansion at its Haridwar facility. With incremental volume expected from the newly commiss
ioned plant, Crisil Ratings expects the company’s revenue to exceed over Rs 3,500–3,700 crore over the medium term.
SMIL could sustain its operating margin at 12.17% in fiscal 2026, signifying its ability to pass on hike in prices. However, the operating margin moderated to ~10% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, as increase in raw material prices is passed on with a lag. Moreover, occurrence of a fire at one of their customer’s supplier led to lower-than-expected sales and reduced cost absorption. Suste
nance of operating margin will be key monitorable.
The financial risk profile remains robust, marked by minimal dependence on debt and large networth of Rs 1,293 crore as on March 31, 2026. The company also maintains liquid investments of over Rs 990 crore.
The ratings continue to reflect SMIL’s established market position, driven by a strong customer profile and market share, healthy operating efficiency and prudent working capital management. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to customer
and product concentration risk, susceptibility to increase in raw material prices and pricing pressure from original equipment manufacturers (OEMs).
Analytical approach
Crisil Ratings has evaluated the consolidated business and financial risk profiles of SMIL with its wholly owned subsidiary Uddipt Mobility India Private Limited.

Key rating drivers - Strengths
Established market position, driven by strong customer profile and market share:
The three-decade-long experience in manufacturing exhaust
systems has enabled the promoter to maintain heathy relationships with major OEMs, and operate as the preferred supplier of independent front suspension systems. Apart from exhaust systems, SMIL manufactures and supplies various suspension systems and trades
in catalytic convertors to its customers.
The company caters to leading manufacturers of PVs, CVs, tractors and off-highway equipment. SMIL holds ~30% market share in India (in value terms) for the PV and light CV segments and has been catering to bot
h domestic and international markets. Being one of India's leading manufacturers of exhaust systems, catalytic converters, diesel particulate filters (DPF), selective catalytic reduction (SCR) systems and other emission-control products, the company also benefits from the stringent emission norms.
Healthy operating efficiency and prudent working capital management:
SMIL has an integrated manufacturing set-up and benefits from strong in-house design and development capabilities and technical support from c
ollaborators. Operating from multi-locational facilities enables better inventory management and reduces freight cost. Strong market position in exhaust systems has helped the company maintain a steady operating margin of 10–12%. Besides, return on capital employed was healthy at ~34 % in fiscal 2026, and is expected to remain above 30% over the medium term.
SMIL had high unencumbered cash balance of over Rs 990
crore as on
March 31, 2026, while the fund-based limit remained unutilised for the 12 months th
rough
September 2026.
Receivables and inventory were in the range of 40–50 days over three fiscals ended March 31, 2026. Working capital cycle was partially
supported by stretched payables of 80–100
days
during fiscals 2024 and 2025; payables are likely to remain at similar levels over the medium
term.
Robust financial risk profile:
Capital structure was strong, as reflected in low total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOL/TNW) ratio of 0.70 time, and healthy networth of Rs 1,293 crore as on Mar
ch 31, 2026, aided by steady accretion to reserve and low debt. Networth is expected to improve, supported by a stable operating margin. With
regular maintenance capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 70–100 crore and absence of any
major debt-funded capex plans, the capital structure should sustain at similar levels.
Debt protection metrics
were robust with interest coverage ratio of
95.49 times and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratio in fiscal
2026; the ratios are expected to remain within a similar range
in fiscal 2027 .
The financial risk profile remains supported by steady cash accrual from operations, adequate liquidity and prudent working capital management. This provides financial flexibility to undertake capex while sustaining growth and withstanding cyclicality in the industry.
Key rating drivers - Weaknesses
Susceptibility to customer and product concentration risk:
SMIL only manufactures exhausts and suspension lines and trades in catalytic convertors for various OEMs.
The top two customers in
the exhaust business contributed to ~75% of total revenue for fiscal 2026. Revenue concentration risk may remain high because of the small product portfolio, and tie-ups made by various OEMs with their vendors for BS-VI products.
SMIL has expertise in exhaust systems since its inception, and it will continue to be a core segment and major revenue contributor (~ 88% in fiscal 2026).
While focus on exhaust systems for CVs shields the company from disruption caused by electric vehicles (EVs), steady rise in
revenue contribution from CVs and gensets will remain monitorable. However, the management's proactive initiatives to launch new products, foster strong relationships with OEMs, and acquire new customers in the domestic and overseas markets, should mitigate the impact of cyclicality in demand and customer concentration risk. The group's ability to sustain revenue growth, diversify its customer and segmental base, and capitalise on its expanded capacity, amidst cyclical industry trends, will be closely monit
ored.
Susceptibility to increase in raw material prices and pricing pressure from OEMs:
The company has limited bargaining power with OEMs, which periodically revise prices based on their financial standing and willingness. As such, any benefit in operating margin comes with a lag.
Strong market position and niche product profile enable SMIL it
to pass on price hikes to customers, although with a lag, supported by healthy and sustained profitability.
Liquidity
Superior
Cash accrual of Rs 300–350 cro
re, expected per fiscal, should support liquidity in the absence of any term debt obligation over the medium term. Current ratio was healthy at 1.92 times as on March 31, 2026. SMIL also held liquid investment of around Rs 990 core in shares, debentures and mutual funds, as on March 31, 2026. Low gearing and moderate networth support financial flexibility and provide cushion in case of any adverse conditions or downturn in the business.
Outlook
Stable
Crisil Ratings
believes SMIL will continue to bene
fit from its established position, healthy relationships with OEMs, and steady demand for passenger cars and utility
vehicles.
Rating sensitivity factors
Upward factors
Sustained improvement in operating income, aided by diverse product profile, and operating margin of over 12%, leading to higher-than-expected net cash accrual
Sustenance of healthy financial risk profile
Downward factors
Degrowth in revenue and operating margin of less than 10%, impacting cash accrual
Large, debt-funded capex or investme
nts, weakening the financial risk profile
About the group
Incorporated in 1986, SMIL is managed by Ajay Relan and is India’s largest manufacturer of exhausts for automotive players.
