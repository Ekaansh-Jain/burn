Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1' ratings on the bank facilities of
Hero Motors Limited
(HML).
The ratings continue to reflect the healthy business risk profile of HML, driven by improving contribution from high value-added components leading to higher margin and increasing revenue diversification over the years. The o
perating income is estimated to have witnessed a low single digit growth in fiscal 2025, supported by domestic markets even as exports contracted due to continued slowdown in the European markets. Further, the adjusted operating margin (after adjusting non-cash share-based ex
pense) witnessed slight improvement over the previous margin of 10.2% in fiscal 2024, driven by increase in the gross margin and benefits of operating leverage. The subsidiaries, that contributed positively to the overall profitability in fiscal 2024, are expected to have witnessed stable operating performance in fiscal 2025, driven by significant ramp-up in Hewland Engineering Ltd. Other subsidiaries are also expected to scale up in the near term and will remain monitorable. The operating performance is ex
pected to improve gradually with pick-up in demand and launch of new products over the medium term and will remain monitorable. Increased revenue contribution from the high-margin segments should lead to gradual margin expansion over the medium term.
The ratings also factor in the strong financial risk profile, with consolidated debt of around Rs 400 crore as on March 31, 2025. To fund growth, the company is expected to undertake capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 300-400 crore over the next 3-4 fiscals th
rough a mix of debt and internal cash accrual.
Crisil Ratings had taken note of the company withdrawing its draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) on October 5, 2024. The company filed the DRHP in August 2024, for a proposed initial public offering of Rs 900 crore. The company is in the process of evaluating the re-filing of DRHP in the near future.
The ratings continue to reflect the healthy business
risk profile of HML, driven by improving revenue diversification and strong financial risk profile despite
the sizeable capex. These strengths are partially offset by investment towards subsidiaries in the ramp-up phase, customer concentration in the revenue profile and exposure to cyclicality in the automotive (auto) components industry.
Analytical Approach
Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of HML, its subsidiaries and joint ventures. This is because all these entities have common management and strong business and financial linkages.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description
Strengths
:
Healthy business risk profile, driven by increasing
revenue diversification
HML has gradually included high value-added components and has built capabilities in more complex parts, leading to significant improvement in the product diversity and operating performance in the recent years. The company continues to diversify its operations from the alloy and metals business to other business lines that fetch incremental margin.
Geographical diversity has improved, with exports contributing to around 40% of t
he revenue in fiscal 2025, compared with a negligible share in fiscal 2018. The overall revenue rose significantly to Rs 1,055 crore in fiscal 2023, from Rs 904 crore in fiscal 2022. However, it remained nearly flat in fiscal 2024 at Rs 1,066 crore and recorded marginal growth in fiscal 2025. However, recovery is expected over the medium term driven by product addition and ramp-up in subsidiaries. Furthermore, the company expects the contribution from the electric vehicles business to increase significantly
over the medium term. The business risk profile may remain healthy, led by growth in the high value-added segments and the addition of customers and products, leading to improving revenue diversity.
Strong financial risk profile despite high capex intensity
:
Consolidated debt stood at around Rs 400 crore against adjusted networth of over Rs 365 crore as on March 31, 2025. The company is expected to undertake capex of Rs 300-400 crore in the next 3-4 fiscals that will be funded through a mix of debt and in
ternal accruals. Debt protection metrics remained healthy in fiscal 2025, as reflected in the estimated interest coverage ratio of over 3 times. Interest coverage ratio is expected at more than 3 times over the medium term.
Weaknesses
:
Investment towards subsidiaries in ramp-up phase
The
company
has
acquired stake in Hewland Engineering Ltd to gain capabilities in the high-performance two-wheeler and four-wheeler segments. Similarly, it has set up other subsidiaries recently,
such as HYM Systems Pvt Ltd and Hero EDU Systems Pvt Ltd,
for expansion into new geographies and product lines. HML also acquired 100% stake in Spur in November 2023 from Hero Cycles Ltd, to cater to existing and new customers of HML. Until fiscal 2023,
the subsidiar
ies had negligible contribution to the operating performance of the company. While Hewland Engineering Ltd witnessed improvement in its operating profitability during fiscal 2024 and is estimated to have registered stable performance in fiscal 2025, other subsidiaries are yet to contribute to the overall operating profitability.
Hence, these subsidiaries will require financial support
for funding their capex in the near term. Ramp-up in operations of the subsidiaries and their contribution to the overall op
erating performance over the medium term will be monitorable.
Customer concentration in revenue and exposure to cyclicality in the auto components industry
Revenue is largely dependent on demand from original equipment manufacturers in the two-wheeler industry, which in turn depends on several factors, such as disposable incomes, consumer preferences, credit availability and fuel prices. Furthermore, while the company has gradually added customers, the top five customers contributed to 60-65% of the revenu
e. This dependence poses a risk to operating performance in case of any significant slowdown.
Liquidity
: Strong
Unencumbered cash and liquid investments stood at around Rs 50 crore as on March 31, 2025. Fund-based limit was utilised at 75-80% during the six months through April 2025. Healthy annual cash accrual, expected at Rs 75-100 crore, will sufficiently cover moderate debt obligation and working capital requirement. The company has significant capex of Rs 300-400 crore planned over the next 3-4 fisc
als, to be funded through mix of debt and internal cash accrual.
Outlook
:
Stable
The business risk profile of HML will remain healthy over the medium term, with addition of customers and products, and ramp-up of subsidiaries. The financial risk profile may continue to be strong despite significant capex of Rs 300-400 crore planned over the next 3-4 fiscals, to be funded through a mix of debt and internal cash accrual.

About the Company
Incorporated in 1998, HML is a part of the OP Munjal group, whose flagship company is Hero Cycles Ltd
(‘
Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’)
. HML manufactures and supplies auto components including gearboxes, continuous variable transmission and other components for the two-wheeler and electric bicycles segments.
The company demerged its auto components business into Hero Cycles Ltd with effect from April 1, 2014.
The auto components division of Hero Cycles Ltd was merged back into HML in December 2022 with effect from
April 1, 2022
.
