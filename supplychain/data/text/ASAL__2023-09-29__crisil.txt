Detailed Rationale
CRISIL Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘CRISIL BBB+/Stable/CRI
SIL A2’ ratings on the bank
laon
facilities of Automotive Stampings and Assemblies Ltd (ASAL).
The ratings continue to reflect strong support from Tata AutoComp Systems Ltd (TACO; 'CRISIL AA/Stable/CRISIL A1+') and the improving business risk profile of the co
mpany. These strengths are partially offset by high product and customer concentration in revenue, limited value addition in products and cyclicality in the automobile industry.
Revenue grew by around 36% on-year to Rs. 828 crore in fiscal 2023 on account
of positive demand environment in the automobile industry. Growth in production volumes of the principal customers Tata Motors Limited (‘TML’; ‘CRISIL AA/Stable/CRISIL A1+’) and Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Limited (‘TMPVL’; ‘CRISIL AA/Stable/CRISIL A1+
’), translated into strong order inflows for ASAL, resulting in continued uptick in sales volumes. Operating margins expanded by 130 basis points (bps) to 3.9% during the said fiscal period on the back of positive operating leverage gains emanating from vo
lume driven growth. Consequently, operating profitability increased to Rs. 33 crore as against Rs. 16 crore reported in fiscal 2022.
During the first quarter of fiscal 2024, turnover grew by 5% on-year to Rs 219 crore and on sequential basis grew by 4%. T
he operating margin for the quarter expanded by 220 bps on-year to 5%; and on a sequential basis, the margin expanded by 20 bps. The upward trend in profitability has been aided by operating leverage gains emanating from consistent growth in volumes. Over
the medium term, revenue growth is expected to mirror industry trend and operating margins are expected to improve owing to positive operating leverage benefits emanating from higher capacity utilizations.
The financial risk was modest despite substantial
balance sheet deleveraging in fiscal 2022 through reduction of debt from asset monetization on account of negative networth and stretched liquidity metrics. As on March 31, 2023, total debt stood at Rs 46 crore, much below peak levels of Rs 157 crore as o
n March 31, 2021. In fiscal 2022, ASAL monetized two land deals for consideration of Rs 113 crore, the proceeds of which were utilized towards reduction of debt. Over the medium term, the company’s reliance on external funding will be limited; overall debt
levels are expected to largely remain stable owing to adequate coverage of internal accrual vis-à-vis capital expenditure (capex) requirement, and shortfall, if any, will be bridged through funding from the parent company or group entities. In addition, o
wing to sustenance of negative net working capital cycle, working capital requirements will be modest. Over the fiscals 2024 and 2025, the company has planned capex investment of Rs 70-80 crore towards capacity enhancement, setting up new capacities and ma
intenance capex.
Analytical Approach
CRISIL Ratings has applied its parent notch-up framework to factor in strong operational, financial and managerial support from TACO, which holds 75% equity stake and has a track record of providing financial suppo
rt by way of unsecured loans and intercorporate deposits.
Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description
Strengths:
Strong business and financial support from TACO and other group companies:
ASAL is a key supplier of sheet-metal stampings, welded assemblie
s and modules for passenger car vehicles and commercial vehicles manufactured by TMPVL and TML, respectively. TML is the largest commercial vehicle player with around 39% market share, whereas TMPVL has the highest market share of around 80% in the passeng
er electric vehicle (EV) segment and is one of the leading players in the passenger vehicles segment with market share of around 12%. The market share of TMPVL and TML is unlikely to be impacted over the medium term aided by improved product portfolio and
demand outlook. Hence, ASAL will benefit from the growth of TML and TMPVL. In addition, TACO has provided intercorporate debt to ASAL to ensure timely debt servicing, which is expected to continue going forward.
Improving operating performance:
The operat
ing performance witnessed a turnaround in fiscal 2022 largely on the back of growth in production volumes of TML and TMPVL driven by positive demand sentiments. Over fiscals 2020 to 2021, the automobile industry witnessed downtrend on account of demand con
traction owing to introduction of BS-VI emissions norms and COVID-19 pandemic induced large scale lockdowns & other related disruptions such as curtailment of consumption and infrastructure spending, labour shortage, among others.
However, in fiscal 2022,
economic activity resumed and strong offtake in demand resulted in revival of automobile demand, with TML and TMPVL leading the way through new product launches, revamped product portfolio, first mover advantage in EV, and high brand value in commercial v
ehicles. The demand momentum continued in fiscal 2023, and over the medium term, though demand sentiments are positive, growth is expected to moderate with easing pent-up demand. ASAL’s revenues mirrored the industry growth trend on the back of TML and TMP
VL’s production volume growth, and over the medium term, turnover is expected to grow in line with the production volume growth of principal customers, and operating margins are expected to improve owing to higher capacity utilization, and thereby resultin
g in better fixed overhead absorption. That said, while the company has the ability to pass on part of the commodity price inflation, margins remain vulnerable to sharp fluctuations in raw material prices.
In addition, on account of balance sheet delevera
ging through debt reduction in fiscal 2022, interest expense burden has reduced, and the company is expected to generate profit after tax (PAT), as against PAT losses registered over fiscal periods 2013 to 2021.
Weakness
es
:
Susceptibility to inherent cycl
icality in the auto industry:
ASAL caters to the automobile industry, which is highly sensitive to macroeconomic events causing economic downturns. Auto original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) were adversely hit in fiscal 2020 owing to drop in consumer spe
nding, weak monsoon, and imposition of BS-VI emission norms. To add further pressure, fiscal 2021 was impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic led disruption. Growth
recovered
only from the second half of fiscal 2021. Hence, performance remains vulnerable to econ
omic downturns largely related to macroeconomic factors.
High customer concentration in revenue:
ASAL derives more than 80% of revenues from TMPVL, TML, and other TACO group companies, exposing the company to revenue concentration risk. While the same is
expected to continue, the company shall look to add new clients to reduce customer concentration.
Weak, albeit improving, financial risk profile
:
The Company’s net-worth has remained negative due to continued losses on account of low operating margins and
high interest burden emanating from sizeable debt levels. A large portion of debt relates to loans & advances received from TATA Group (TACO, Tata Hendrickson, Tata Capital, etc.). However, owing to improved cash accruals in fiscal 2022, driven by improved
operating performance, and one-off asset monetization, the company was able to de-leverage its balance sheet by repaying debt of around Rs. 113 crore and thereby bring down debt levels to Rs. 41 crore during fiscal 2022 as against peak debt levels of Rs.
157 crore during fiscal 2021.
As on March 31, 2023, total debt levels was stable at around Rs. 46 crore. The debt recorded in the balance sheet pertains to inter-corporate deposits received from parent company ‘TACO’. Over the medium term, the company’s
reliance on external funding is expected to be limited and overall debt levels are expected to remain stable owing to improving cash accruals and adequacy of the same to cover the major portion of capex requirements. In addition, working capital requiremen
ts are expected to be minimal due to sustenance of net negative working capital cycle.
Liquidity
:
Adequate
Expected net cash accruals over the fiscal period 2024 to 2025 shall be able to cover capex requirements totaling Rs. 70-80 crore over the said fisc
al periods, and shortfall if any, shall be bridged through debt from parent company or group entities. In addition, working capital requirements are also expected to remain modest on account of the sustenance of the negative net working capital cycle. Furt
hermore, the company also has access to Rs. 19 crore fund-based limits, which remain unutilized during the last twelve months through to August 2023.
Outlook
:
Stable
CRISIL Ratings believes ASAL will continue to benefit from the regular funding support
from TACO though the business risk profile will be constrained by modest operating efficiency metrics. In addition, the financial risk profile, though continues to remain weak, is expected to improve on the back of improving operating efficiency.

ng Sensitivity factors

Improvement in ratings of parent company.
Substantial increase in operating profitability, with operating margins above 8% on sustained basis.
Sustained improvement in financial risk profile.
Downward factors

nificant decline in revenue, or operating margins declining below 3% on sustained basis.

About
the Company
ASAL was set up as JBM Tools Ltd (JBM) by SK Arya and Associates (SKAA) in March 1990 and got its current name in August 2003. ASAL manufactures sheet-metal stampings, welded assemblies and modules for passenger cars and commercial vehicles,
largely for TML; these products account for more than 95% of revenue. It has manufacturing facilities in Pune and Pantnagar, Uttarakhand.
ASAL went public in March 1994, and TACO, a Tata group company, became a joint venture (JV) partner in 1997. In Apri
l 2002, SKAA exited the JV and transferred its entire holding in JBM to TACO and Tata Industries Ltd ('CRISIL A1+').
In February 2007, TACO entered into an agreement with Gestamp Servicios S L (Gestamp) under which both the companies were to hold equal e
quity stakes in ASAL. Consequently, Gestamp acquired 0.01% stake through an open offer, and TACO transferred 37.49% of its stake in ASAL to Gestamp. In February 2007, TACO reduced its stake to 37.5% (same as Gestamp), while the remaining shares were owned
by the public and others. With the purchase of Gestamp's stake in December 2010, TACO now holds 75% stake in ASAL.
