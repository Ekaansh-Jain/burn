Rationale
India’s exporters have been navigating a turbulent trade environment, following the high level of tariffs imposed by the US.
The latest round of US tariffs, effective August 27, 2025, has added a punitive 25% duty on select Indian goods, significantly
increasing the overall tariff burden and creating cost pressure. The US has also levied tariffs on steel, aluminum, copper and
their derivative products under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act.
GNA Axles Ltd. (GAL) generates 20-25% of its overall revenues from the US with most of its products falling under the Section
232 tariff categories, exposing it to elevated tariffs of 50% for commercial vehicles (CV)/ tractors segment and 25% for the light
vehicles (LV) segment.
However, as indicated by the management, GAL’s 50-60% of exports to the US are on an ex-works basis, wherein the import
duty is borne by the customer upon import into the US. For the balance, exports are on a delivery duty paid (DDP) basis,
wherein the import duty is borne by GAL, with an eventual pass through to the customers. GAL is expected to continue to pass
on the costs arising out of higher tariffs to its customers through increasing realisations of the parts exported, which is likely
to mitigate the impact of cost pressure on its profitability. Moreover, the substitution risk for domestic auto component
manufacturers supplying to the US-based original equipment manufacturers (OEM) and tier-1 suppliers are low owing to
elongated product development cycle and approval/ trial processes.
Notwithstanding some moderation in revenue due to lower exports, given the uncertain trade environment in the US market,
GAL's performance remained largely stable in Q1 FY2026 despite application of initial 25% derivative tariffs from March 2025
(further increased to 50% from June 3, 2025), indicating steady supplies and the ability of the company to pass on the increased
costs. The company’s operating profit margin (OPM) improved to 14.6% in Q1 FY2026 against 13.3% in FY2025 supported by
continued raw material pass-through and cost rationalisation initiatives. The company also maintains an adequate liquidity
position, supported by a cushion of Rs. 50 crore against sanctioned working capital limits and Rs. 20-30 crore of free cash and
bank balances as of August 2025, supporting its credit profile. Additionally, GAL’s established track record in the industry, long
relations with its customers and a healthy financial risk profile marked by a comfortable capital structure and debt coverage
metrics further provide comfort to withstand any implications arising out of the ongoing trade environment. Nonetheless,
ICRA will continue to monitor the evolving situation and its implications for GAL's credit profile.

About the company
GAL, incorporated in 1993, manufactures forged and machined automotive transmission components such as spindles, drive
shafts and axles. GAL is one of the leading auto components and parts providers to major original equipment manufacturers
and tier-1 suppliers, with major presence in the CV and tractor segments in both the domestic as well as the export markets.
The company, promoted by Mr. Gursaran Singh and family, has integrated manufacturing units at Hoshiarpur, Punjab, with an
installed production capacity of 6.7 million pieces per annum. GAL was listed on the National Stock Exchange and the Bombay
Stock Exchange on September 26, 2016.
