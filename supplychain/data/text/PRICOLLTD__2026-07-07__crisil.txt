Crisil Ratings has
placed its rating on
the long-term bank facilities

The rating action follows the announcement by Pricol at the stock exchanges on June 27, 2026, that its board of directors has approved the scheme of arrangement between Pricol and Pricol Autotech Ltd (Pricol Autotech, presently a wholly-owned subsidiary of Pricol) and their respective shareholders and creditors. The scheme involves the demerger of the Driver Information and Connected Vehicle Solutions (DICVS) business to Pricol Autotech. The DICVS b
usiness contributed Rs 2,424.63 crore, representing 61.17% of Pricol’s consolidated revenue in fiscal 2026.
As a result of this demerger, the existing shareholders will receive one share of Pricol Autotech for every one share held in Pricol; hence, both entities will have mirror shareholding, with promoters holding ~38.5% stake in both entities. The scheme is, inter alia, subject to receipt of statutory, regulatory and customary approvals, including approvals from the stock exchanges, National Company Law
Tribunal, Chennai bench and the shareholders and creditors. The process is expected to take 15–18 months to complete, and the effective date for the scheme will be April 1, 2027. The rating watch will be resolved upon completion of the demerger process and as further clarity emerges on the respective profit and loss account and balance sheets, and future business and capital expenditure (capex) plans for both entities. Any moderation in the credit profile of Pricol due to the demerger process will remain a
key rating sensitivity factor.
Pricol’s consolidated revenue rose to Rs 4,041 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 2,695 crore in fiscal 2025, primarily driven by the addition of the injection moulded plastic component solutions (IMPCS) business, which was acquired by its subsidiary, Pricol Precision Products Pvt Ltd (P3L; ‘Crisil AA-/Watch Developing’), in January 2025 from Sundaram Auto Components Ltd (SACL, a subsidiary of TVS Motor Company Ltd).
Furthermore, the traditional business of Prico
l also grew ~22%, primarily supported by two-wheeler (2W) volume growth of ~22.9% during the period, change in the product mix driven by reforms in goods and service tax (GST) structure and healthy exports. Over the medium term, Crisil Ratings expects Pricol’s revenue to grow in healthy double digits, supported by continued demand from original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), launch of new products, continued improvement in the product mix and addition of new business.
Pricol’s operating margin moderated t
o 11.6% in fiscal 2026 from 11.9% in the previous corresponding period, impacted by a modest increase in raw material prices and addition of a low-margin business at the subsidiary. Nevertheless, after acquisition of the P3L business by Pricol, its operating margin improved to 9.13% in fiscal 2026, supported by productivity enhancements, reduction in rejection rates and lower employee cost. P3L is also replacing some of its old machines and implementing various cost optimisation measures, which are expected
to further support improvement in operating profitability.
Although the operating margin of the traditional business remains susceptible to changes in raw material prices, despite having a passthrough clause with its customers, it is expected to remain stable at 11-12% annually, supported by improvement in the product mix and continued cost optimisation measures.
Pricol’s financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy, driven by improving networth and moderate debt level, supported by prudent worki
ng capital management and capex. Pricol’s debt, including acceptances, was Rs 363 crore as on March 31, 2026, compared with Rs 269 crore as on March 31, 2025. The company is planning capex of Rs 500 crore for the current fiscal, which will be partly funded through healthy cash accrual of Rs 370–390 crore and the balance through debt.
Debt level is expected to peak in the current fiscal; however, leverage metrics such as gearing are expected to remain below 0.5 time, while debt protection metrics such as in
terest coverage and debt to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) ratios are expected to remain healthy at 10–11 times and ~1.5 times, respectively, over the medium term, which is marginally higher than earlier anticipated.
Besides annual cash generation, liquidity is also supported by cash surplus of ~Rs 126 crore as on March 31, 2026, and adequate headroom in bank limits of Rs 105 crore, which were utilised at over 43% for the six months through May 2026. Any further inorg
anic growth opportunities, necessitating material debt addition and impacting the debt protection metrics, will remain a key rating sensitivity factor.
The rating on the debt instruments of Pricol reflects the company’s established business risk profile, supported by healthy relationships with domestic OEMs, leadership position in 2W instrument clusters and improving revenue and product diversity due to industry transition from mechanical clusters to light-emitting diode (LED) and thin-film transistor (TFT
) clusters. The rating is also supported by benefits arising from the recent acquisition, improving operating efficiency and financial risk profile.
These strengths are partially offset by revenue concentration in 2W OEMs and high dependence on imported raw materials, which render profitability vulnerable to adverse foreign exchange (forex) movements.

Crisil Ratings has consolidated Pricol’s financials with its wholly owned subsidiaries, P3L,
PT Pricol Surya – Indonesia and Pricol Asia Pte Ltd, Singapore, since all these entities are in the same business with significant business and financial linkages. Crisil Ratings has also consolidated Pricol Asia Exim DMCC, a wholly owned subsidiary of Pricol Asia Pte Ltd.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Leading position in 2W instrument clusters and improving product and customer diversity, supported by healthy relationships with OEMs
Pricol enjoys a healthy market position in dashboard instruments and driver information systems with a track record of about five decades in supplying to leading domestic OEMs. On standalone basis, revenue share of dashboard instruments was ~71% in fiscal 2026 owing to improvement in the product mix, leading to better realisations. Pumps and mechanical products contributed 19
% to the revenue in fiscal 2026, while switches and sensors formed the balance. Presence in different segments adds to product diversity. The company has in-house research and development (R&D) and a track record of launching new products quickly. It plans to add new products to its portfolio, such as handlebars and battery management systems, which will further enhance its product basket.
Globally, Pricol is the second-largest instrument cluster maker in terms of volume, next to Nippon Seiki & Co Ltd, Jap
an. Pricol also holds 55-60% market share in the domestic instrument cluster business, and 65% market share in 2W instrument clusters.
Majority of the revenue comes from domestic OEMs (~90% in fiscal 2026) with relatively modest aftermarket and exports presence. The 2W OEMs contribute ~70% to the revenue and the company has healthy relationships with key OEMs such as TVSM, Hero MotoCorp Ltd (‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Bajaj Auto Ltd (‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Eicher Motors Ltd (Royal Enfield
2Ws) and commercial vehicle (CV) OEMs - Tata Motors Ltd (‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and Ashok Leyland Ltd, and construction equipment major, JCB India Ltd (‘Crisil AAA/Stable’). The company earlier had a non-compete agreement with its erstwhile joint venture partner, Denso Corporation, Japan, which recently expired, following which Pricol can supply to passenger vehicle (PV) OEMs.
Pricol to benefit from the acquisition of IMPCS division
Pricol’s segmental diversification is set to significantly impro
ve with its dependence on the dashboard segment (~75% share of revenue in fiscal 2025) reducing to ~54% in fiscal 2026, with sizeable contribution of over Rs 923 crore from the IMPCS business. P3L’s revenue for this fiscal is expected to cross Rs 1,000 crore, driven by new business, with further improvement in operating profitability to 9.5-10.0% from 9.13% in fiscal 2026, driven by productivity enhancements, reduction in rejection rates to minimal levels and lower employee cost.
A sizeable portion of Pri
col’s and P3L’s products are electric vehicle (EV) agnostic; hence the impact on revenue due to a gradual shift towards EV will not be material and revenue loss will be more than offset by new product launches expected over the medium term.
Improving operating efficiency
Pricol has strong R&D capabilities and has started
manufacturing more critical components in-house, which ensures better quality and less wastage. Profitability was boosted by the company’s focus on automation, which allowed for material p
runing of the workforce, enhancing share of wallet with existing customers and moving up the value chain. Hiving off the loss-making overseas subsidiaries and change in product mix in favour of better premium price complex products has also contributed to increase in operating margin from fiscal 2021 onwards. However, on account of moderate increase in raw material prices and acquisition of the low-margin IMPCS division in January 2025, the consolidated margin moderated
to 11.85% in fiscal 2025 and further
to 11.62% in fiscal 2026. Nevertheless, P3L’s operating margin
improved to 9.13% during the last fiscal, primarily driven by various cost optimisation measures, increased operating leverage, improved productivity through replacement of old machines and new business from OEMs. Improvement in operating margin at P3L, along with healthy profitability from the existing business, is expected to keep Pricol’s consolidated margin steady at ~11-12% over the medium term.
Operations were impacted by labour issues a
nd strikes in the past; however, there have been cordial relationships in recent years. Any major labour issue impacting operations will be monitorable.
Healthy financial risk profile
Strong cash accrual since fiscal 2021 and a rights issue of Rs 81 crore in fiscal 2021, enabled debt reduction, strengthening the balance sheet and debt protection metrics. Gearing was 0.32 time in fiscal 2026 compared with 0.22 time in fiscal 2025, as the company had availed debt of Rs 127 crore to fund the acquisition of th
e IMPCS division and increase working capital debt to Rs 236 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 114 crore as on March 31, 2025. The company incurred capex of around ~Rs 323 crore during fiscal 2026 which was partly funded through debt, leading to increase in total debt to Rs 363 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 269 crore as on March 31, 2025. For the current fiscal, capex is likely to be ~Rs 500 crore which will be partly funded through debt. Debt levels are expected to peak in the current fiscal reachi
ng Rs 550-600 crore, but is expected to come down subsequently with progressive repayments. Gearing is expected to peak at ~0.4-0.5 time in the current fiscal and improve over the medium term, while debt to Ebitda ratio is expected to remain below 1.3 times. Nevertheless, debt protection metrics such as interest coverage and net cash accrual to total debt ratios will remain healthy at above 10 times and 0.5-0.7 time over the medium term, respectively.
Key Rating Drivers -
We
a
knesses
Revenue dependence on the 2W segment
Domestic OEMs contribute 89-90% to the revenue, with exports contributing ~5% while the aftermarket segment makes up the balance. The company has no significant share in the aftermarket segment due to long lifecycle of products. Similarly, export revenue share is minimal on account of strong competition from global giants. High exposure to domestic OEMs makes Pricol vulnerable to the auto demand cycle and the consequent offtake by customers. Pricol has improved its segm
ental revenue with 2Ws contributing ~70%. However, any prolonged slowdown in 2W demand, as was witnessed during fiscals 2020-2022, impacts offtake for components.
Revenue dependence on the 2W segment may continue although Pricol has taken initiatives to improve its share in the PV segment (8-9% of sales in fiscal 2026), where it is already in discussions with some OEMs. Besides, improved demand from CVs will help reduce revenue dependence on the 2W segment. However, given its strong relationships with 2W
OEMs and leading position in the instrument cluster business, revenue concentration on OEMs and the 2W segment will persist over the medium term.
High import dependence rendering profitability vulnerable to adverse forex movements
The company imports 45-50% of its raw materials (LCD and TFT screens) against ~40% earlier, which was driven by increased imports in the TFT segment. Of this, 65-70% is from China while other import sources include South Korea, Japan and Taiwan. All imports are routed through it
s subsidiary in Singapore, Pricol Asia Pte Ltd. The high import content in its raw material mix and limited hedging activity exposes profitability to fluctuations in forex and freight costs, especially during volatile global markets.
Liquidity
Strong
Liquidity is supported by healthy cash generation of Rs 370-390 crore over the medium term, besides cash balance of ~Rs 126 crore as on March 31, 2026. While the cash accrual is expected to be adequate for meeting the debt obligation of Rs 25-30 crore and Rs 40-50 crore, respectively, in this and next fiscals, and incremental working capital requirement, Pricol is expected to avail debt of Rs 200-250 crore in this and next fiscals for capex of Rs 500 crore at a consolidated level. The company also h
as access to bank limit of Rs 105 crore (utilised at ~43% on average during the six months through May 2026).

About the Company
Pricol commenced operations in 1975 in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, and is currently one of India's leading dashboard manufacturers. The company offers driver information systems and sensors, pumps and allied products, telematics and wiping systems catering to leading auto OEMs in 2W and 3W, PV, CV, farm equipment and offroad vehicle segments across India and overseas (45+ countries) with more than 2,000 product variants. As on date, the company had 15 manufacturing plants (including subsidiaries) across India (
Coimbatore, Manesar, Pantnagar, Pune, Sricity, Mysuru, Hosur,
Narasapura, Chennai, Nalagarh, Bhiwadi and Sanand), one manufacturing unit in Indonesia, and three international offices (Dubai, Tokyo and Singapore).
On June 30, 2026, the promoters, Vikram Mohan and his family members, held 38.5% stake in Pricol, foreign portfolio investors held 13.95%, mutual funds held 9.86%, alternate investment funds held 1.89%, and public and others the balance shareholding.
