Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has downgraded its ratings on the long-term bank facilities and commercial paper of Munjal Showa Limited (MSL) to
‘Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+’
from ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’ and has simultaneously
withdrawn
its rating on the Rs 6 crore commercial paper programme as requested by the company. The withdrawal is in line with the Crisil Ratings policy on withdrawal of ratings.
The rating downgrade follows sustained moderation in the business risk profile of the company as reflected in lower-than-expected profitability, with revenue remaining rangebound. The business risk profile remains constrained by limited customer diversification, rising competitive intensity and low bargaining power with original equipment m
anufacturers (OEMs). Revenue rose by ~7% on-year in fiscal 2025 to Rs 1,250 crore on account of increased demand from its key customer’s models. However, the operating margin continued to be low, at 1.4%, during fiscal 2025, against 1.35% in fiscal 2024. Furthermore, the company reported revenue of Rs 286 crore, with 8% on-year decline and 6% quarter-on-quarter decline in the first quarter of fiscal 2026 due to fall in sales volume of its primary customers. The revenue growth shall remain flat to low single
-digit in the medium term. Growth in the scale of operations has been constrained owing to high customer concentration and inability to diversify the customer and product profile, which also resulted in a slower-than-expected ramp-up in performance leading to lower capacity utilisation and sustained low profitability.
Operating margin remained flat at 1.4% in fiscal 2025 owing to lower operating leverage and one-time provisions. However, the company has taken multiple cost corrective measures to align its
profitability. Cost corrective measures such as change in standard operating procedures (SOP), allotment of voluntary retirement scheme (VRS) to the employees and investments in solar power will lead to some improvement in operating margin. Margin in the first quarter of fiscal 2026 declined sharply to ~0.5% on account of significant lower offtake of volume of its key customers impacting the operating leverage of the company, and higher input cost incurred, which is yet to be passed on to the customers.
De
spite a modest business risk profile, the ratings centrally derive comfort from strong financial risk profile, backed by strong networth of Rs 673 crore as on March 31, 2025, and a debt free balance sheet. Low reliance on debt has led to strong gearing of less than 0.1 time since fiscal 2013. Liquidity has also remained strong with unencumbered liquidity surplus of ~Rs 283 crore (including investments in mutual funds of Rs 257 crore). Additionally, the company has an investment of Rs 92 crore in debentures,
bonds and units of alternate investment fund as on March 31, 2025.
Annual net cash accrual of Rs 15–19 crore over the medium term will be sufficient to meet the capital expenditure (capex) requirement of Rs 6–7 crore per annum, as well as working capital needs over the medium term. Furthermore, the company has access to working capital limits of Rs 63.5 crore, which are minimally utilised, further supporting the strong liquidity.
Analytical Approach
Crisil Ratings has considered the
standalone
business
and
financial risk profiles of MSL.
Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description
Strengths:
Established
business linkages with key customers and above-average scale of operations
:
MSL
is among the key players in the domestic shock-absorber industry and one of the leading suppliers to
Hero MotoCorp Ltd (HMCL; rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), one of the largest player in the two-wheeler market in India. Earlier, MSL used to
supply shock absorbers to Honda Motorcycle and Scooters India Ltd (HMSI). However, it has discontinued
this due to
change in the client’s sourcing policy. MSL also
supplies
struts and window balances to Maruti Suzuki India Ltd (rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil
A1+’).
HMCL accounts for 80–85% of the total revenue.
Healthy
financial risk profile
:
Networth was adequate at Rs 673
crore and
the balance sheet continued to be debt free as on March 31, 2025. Despite low profitability, debt protection metrics remained healthy due to nil debt. Though the cash accrual is expected to remain modest owing
to subdued operational efficiency, it will be adequate to meet capex (Rs 6–7 crore) and working capital requirement. Liquidity is supported by healthy unencumbered liquidity surplus (Rs 283 crore as on March 31, 2025), as well as minimally utilised fund-based
bank limit of Rs 63.5 crore. Reliance on debt is expected to remain minimal over the medium term as well.
Weaknesses
:
Modest operating efficiency
:
Due to a decline in orders across segments and customers concentration, the capacity
utilisation is low (50–60%) leading to lower absorption of fixed costs. This is compounded by low bargaining power with OEMs, leading to inability to pass on increased input costs to customers. These factors constrained the operating margin to ~1.4% in fiscal 2025. However, MSL has taken various cost corrective measures like change in their SOP, allotment of VRS to its employees and installation of solar power plant. These
measures will help sustain profitability at 1.8–2.1% over the medium term.
However, MSL benefits from the fact that its three large manufacturing facilities are close to key clients, which allows it to manage their requirements efficiently.
Limited segmental and geographical diversification
:
Despite increasing supplies to other OEMs, bulk of the revenue is derived
from HMCL (more than 80%).
MSL has negligible reach in the global market due to restrictions from
its collaborator and second-largest shareholder,
Hitachi Astemo Ltd (priorly known as Showa Corporation). Also, it has no direct presence in the high-margin aftermarket segment. Earlier, HMSI had accounted for a healthy share of business (20% of revenue in fiscal 2013), but this declined as Showa India Pvt Ltd
(100% subsidiary of Hitachi
Astemo Ltd)
replaced
MSL
as
the largest supplier of HMSI.
Ability to diversify revenue base and increase supplies to electric two-wheeler OEMs will be critical for revenue growth as HMCL has also been inducting new suppliers for shock absorbers.
Low pricing power against
OEMs
:
The company has limited flexibility to pass on any change in input cost and increase in other manufacturing overheads to customers. Moreover, high dependence on OEMs and exposure to intense competition would continue to constrain
profitability, as seen in the past 3–4 fiscals.
Liquidity
: Strong
Liquidity remained strong with cash
surplus of around Rs 283 crore as on March 31, 2025. Furthermore, presence of minimally utilised fund-based working capital limits of Rs
63.5 crore provides additional cushion. Annual net cash accrual of Rs 15–19 crore would be sufficient to meet yearly capex of Rs 6–7 crore as well as working capital requirement.
Outlook
:
Stable
The company’s performance
will remain modest
over the medium term, given
the high customer concentration and low product diversification. Financial
risk profile will, however, continue to be healthy over the medium term, supported by
high liquidity surplus, sufficient
cash accrual
and
modest capex
spending.
Rating
s
ensitivity factors
Upward
factors:
Substantial growth in revenue, including through customer and product diversification
Improvement in operating margin to 4–5% benefitting cash generation
Downward
factors:
Steady pressure on revenue growth coupled with operating margins sustaining below 1% on sustained basis impacting cash generation
Weaker-than-expected gearing led by large, debt-funded capex/acquisition, or substantial decline in liquid surplus
About the Company
MSL was set up by the erstwhile Hero group in 1985, in technical and financial collaboration with Showa Corporation, Japan. After the family arrangement in May 2010 within the Munjal family, shares of MSL continue to be vested with Mr Yogesh Chander Munjal within the Satyanand Munjal faction.
The Yogesh Chander Munjal family, through Dayanand Munjal Investments Pvt Ltd and Hitachi Astemo Ltd, had equity holdings of 40.10% and 24.90%, respectively, in MSL as on June 30, 2025. The company’s
main products are front forks and shock absorbers, which it supplies to the two-wheeler segment. It also manufactures struts and window balancers for the four-wheeler segment. The facilities are in Gurugram and Manesar (both in Haryana) and Haridwar (Uttarakhand).
