Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities and
commercial paper programme of NRB Bearings L
imited
(NRB).
NRB derives 65-70% of its revenue from domestic automotive (auto) original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), 10-11% from
the
domestic aftermarket and 20-25%
from
exports. During fiscal 2026
, revenue grew 11.3% on-year to Rs 1,336 crore, driven by healthy demand from domestic auto sector, specifically post-revision in GST (Good and Services Tax) rates.
R
evenue is expected to grow at steady rate over the medium term, driven by focus on component categories fetching higher realisations, technology agnostic products, steady demand from the auto segment and launching of new products for the aerospace/defense segment and industrial bearings. The
company has acquired Mahant Tool Room and formed a jo
int venture (JV) with Italy-based Unitec S.r.L (part of the Mondial group) to diversify its product base.
Operating margin increased to 19.4% in fiscal 2026 from 17.7% in fiscal 2025
owing to implementation of cost-saving measures, including higher solar energy usage, increased automation and renegotiation of vendor costs across logistics, insurance and material procurement as well as certain foreign exchange gains. Gross margin sustained at stable level of 54-55% during fiscal 2026
. Operating margins are e
xpected to remain broadly resilient despite elevated raw material costs on the back of the company's ability to pass on a portion of these increases to customers albeit with a lag.
Financial risk profile remains healthy, as seen in networth of Rs 980 crore as on March 31, 2026, against Rs 933 crore as on March 31, 2025. Consolidated debt (including lease liabilities) reduced to Rs 154 crore as on March 31, 2026 (from Rs 187 crore previous fiscal), due to better working capital management. With reduction in
debt, gearing improved to 0.16 time from 0.20 time. Capital expenditure (capex) of around Rs 400 crore is expected to be incurred spread over three fiscals, which will be funded through a mix of debt of Rs 150 crore and expected internal accrual of Rs 140-200 crore. As a result, gearing is expected to increase, but remain healthy, at 0.3 time over the medium term. With reduction in debt during fiscal 2026, interest coverage ratio increased to 25.3 times from 20.1 times a year earlier. With rise in external
borrowings to fund capex and meet additional working capital requirement and sustained profitability, the ratio shall remain above 12 times over the medium term. Furthermore, the company has comfortable liquidity with unencumbered cash surplus of Rs 77 crore as on March 31, 2026.

The ratings continue to reflect NRB’s market leadership in the need
le and cylindrical roller bearing segments, diverse customer profile and strong technical expertise. These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement, vulnerability to cyclicality in the end-user industry and pricing pressure from OEMs.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Market leadership in the needle and cylindrical roller bearing segments in India
The company has an estimated market share of 65-70% in the needle bearing industry in India, with strong presence in cylindrical rollers, special-tapered rollers and special ball bearings. This is aided by the promoter’s extensive industry experience, robust in-house re
search and development facilities and strong client relationships. Currently, the industrial bearing business contributes 14-15% of the overall revenue while catering to industries such as defense, railways, tractors and farm equipment and construction equipment. With the company’s foraying in aerospace and forming a JV with Unitec, this proportion is expected to gradually increase to 20-25% over the medium term.
Diverse customer profile
NRB has longstanding relationships with Indian and global auto OEMs a
nd Tier-1
vendors. It has customers across segments, including two-wheelers, commercial vehicles, passenger cars, utility vehicles, farm equipment and tractors, off-highway vehicles, railways and defense. Domestic OEMs accounted for majority of the revenue (~70%) in fiscal 2026, while aftermarket and export sales contributed the rest. Despite the large proportion of revenue from OEMs, no single customer with its tier 1 supplier accounts for more than 7% of the total revenue, thereby limiting customer concen
tration risk. Furthermore, the company is planning to add clients across newer segments such as aerospace, which will further diversify the clientele base.
Strong technical expertise
NRB has been operating in India for over six decades, during which it has built strong technical expertise and knowhow covering the full range of bearing design, from conception to software-aided simulation, testing, validation, benchmarking and production. The product range spans over 3,000 designs, which are also made to sui
t its customer requirements. The company has a dedicated engineering and design centre in Turbhe, Navi Mumbai. Besides, its plants use automated production and assembly machinery designed in-house, reflecting the strong technical capabilities of its team.
Healthy financial risk profile
Steady rise in profitability increased networth to Rs 980 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 933 crore as on March 31, 2025. Capital structure remains healthy, with gearing of 0.16 time against 0.2 time. Consolidated debt,
including lease liabilities, declined to Rs 154 crore from Rs 187 crore.

Key Rating Drivers - We
a
knesses
Large working capital requirement
Due to the just-in-time delivery to OEMs and
large number of product stock-keeping units across more than 500 products, inventory is high at 130-170 days over
the past five fiscals. Furthermore, payment terms from the auto component manufacturers vary from 60-90 days, resulting in receivables of 60-90 days. Also, the
company imports 30-35% of the key raw material (steel) from Europe (mainly Germany), Japan and other countries. Considering a transit time of 90 days and to avoid the impact of supply chain issues arising from geopolitical issues, the company is required to stock up a huge inventory.
Vulnerability to cyclical demand in the auto component segment and
pricing pressure from OEMs
Bearing manufacturers remain inherently exposed to the cyclicality of the automotive sector due to their significant dependence on vehicle production volumes and end-market demand. While the industry's capital-intensive operations, technological complexities, and entrenched supplier-OEM relationships provide a certain degree of pricing flexibility, input cost pass-throughs are typically implemented with a lag owing to periodic price negotiations, creating temporary pressure on ma
rgins. Furthermore, during prolonged downturns in the automotive market, OEMs often face limitations in fully transferring cost increases to end customers, resulting in a shared absorption of inflationary pressures across the value chain. Concurrently, weaker demand can lead to lower capacity utilisation and under-absorption of fixed overheads, thereby adversely impacting the profitability and operating leverage of auto-component manufacturers. The risk will be partially mitigated with the rising proportion
of revenue from bearings for industrial segment.
Liquidity
Strong
Cash ac
crual is expected at Rs 140-200 crore per annum, sufficient to meet the annual debt obligation of Rs 25 crore over the medium term. This, coupled with anticipated debt of Rs 150 crore, will be sufficient to meet annual capex of Rs 120-160 crore over the medium term. Liquidity is also supported by cash surplus of Rs 77 crore as on March 31, 2026. Furthermore, the fund-based working capital limit of Rs 442 crore at standalone lev
el remains utilised at about 9% for the 12 months through March 31, 2026. The buffer in the form of unutilised limit shall provide sufficient cushion in case of exigencies.

About
the Company
Founded by the late Mr Trilochan Singh Sahney in 1965, NRB manufactures needle, cylindrical, special ball, and taper-roller bearings. It also makes other friction solution components such as planetary shafts, crank pins and kingpins. The company, as of June 2026, has four subsidiaries: NRB Holdings Ltd (holding company of NRB Bearings Europe GmbH, NRB Bearings (Thailand) Ltd and NRB Bearings USA Inc), SNL Bearings Ltd (SNL) and
Mahant Tool Room Pvt Ltd; and a JV,
NRB Unitech Friction Solutions
P Ltd
.
NRB's manufacturing facilities are in Aurangabad, Jalna and Waluj in Maharashtra; Hyderabad; and Pantnagar in Uttarakhand. SNL's facility is in Ranchi in Jharkhand, while NRB Thailand's plant is at Rayong in Thailand. The product engineering centre at Turbhe and process and advanced materials-based engineering centre in Waluj are government accredited.
Ms Harshbeena S Zaveri, daughter of Mr Trilochan Singh Sahney, is the vice-chairman and managing director of NRB and the chairman of the board of SNL
.
