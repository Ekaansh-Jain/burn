Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its
'Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1'
ratings on the bank
loan
facilities of Rane (Madras) Ltd (RML; part of the Rane group).
The reaffirmation reflects the
healthy market position of RML in the domestic automotive steering components segment, its diversified revenue profile and benefits from being part of the Rane group. The ratings also factor in the improving financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by the sizeable investment in the domes
tic diecasting business and modest though improving operating profitability. RML also remains susceptible to cyclicality in demand and pricing pressure arising from sizeable exposure to original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) in the automobile industry.
RML's revenue grew by 7.9% to around Rs.1800 crore in the first half of fiscal 2026, compared to Rs. 1667 crore in the same period of fiscal 2025. This growth was primarily driven by sustained demand from domestic original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), pa
rticularly in the passenger vehicle and farm tractor segments, which witnessed higher offtake. Additionally, exports experienced growth, fueled by strong demand for steering products and steady aftermarket sales. The revenue distribution was as follows: 58% from domestic OEMs, 27% from exports, and 15% from aftermarket segments. Within the OEM segment, passenger vehicles accounted for the majority of revenue at over 57%, followed by commercial vehicles (21%), tractors (10%), and two/three wheelers (6%). Loo
king ahead, RML expects its revenue to grow by 6-8% over the medium term, driven by steady demand from domestic OEMs, new product launches, facelift models, rural demand, and the benefits of recent GST rate reductions.
RML's operating profitability improved to 8.53% in the first half of fiscal 2026, up from 8.2% in the same period of the previous fiscal. This improvement was driven by cost optimization measures implemented following the merger of Rane group entities. However, the gains were partially offse
t by a moderate increase in raw material costs. Looking ahead, the company expects its operating margin to further improve to around 9% over the medium term, driven by benefits from recent consolidation, ongoing cost-efficient measures, a favorable product mix, and improving operating leverage.
Earlier, RML through a scheme of arrangement wherein two listed entities of the Rane group, Rane Engine Valve Ltd (REVL) and Rane Brake Linings Ltd (RBLL) were amalgamated into RML on a share-swap basis. The merger
expanded the product basket offered by RML and changed the segmental diversification compared to pre-merger operations. During fiscal 2025, RML’s revenue stood at
Rs 3406 crore and operating margin 8.9%. The merger scheme improved the financial risk profile of RML, boosting its networth to Rs 663 crore as on March 31, 2025 from Rs 250 crore a year earlier. The balance sheet further strengthened on account of modest debt at REVL and debt-free operations at RBLL, providing upliftment to the debt metrics of R
ML. For instance, ratios such as gearing and debt to earnings before interest tax depreciation and amortization (ebitda) improved to 1.15 and 2.50 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025 from 2.8 and 4.25 times, respectively, a year earlier.
RML aims to reduce its consolidated debt, which stood at Rs. 780 crore as of September 30, 2025 (down from Rs. 761 crore as of March 31, 2025), over the medium term through a combination of sustained debt repayments and the sale of non-core assets. In June 2025, the
company entered into a definitive agreement to sell its 3.48-acre facility and premises in Velachery to Canopy Living LLP, a joint venture between Arihant Foundations & Housing Ltd and Prestige Estates Projects Ltd, for a total consideration of Rs. 361 crore. As part of the deal, RML will retain 1.02 acres of land in Velachery, which will be used for constructing its new offices after obtaining necessary approvals. The company has already received Rs. 115 crore from the sale, which was utilized to reduce i
ts long-term debt. The remaining sale proceeds of Rs. 246 crore are expected to be received over the current and next fiscal years, with approximately Rs. 140 crore earmarked for long-term debt reduction and the balance allocated for office construction. Additionally, RML plans to incur annual capital expenditure of around Rs. 200 crore for maintenance and modernization purposes, which will be funded through its cash accruals
.
RML's cash accrual from operations is expected to steadily improve over the nex
t few fiscal years. For the current and next fiscal years, cash accrual is expected to be sufficient to meet moderate repayment obligations, with a significant portion of long-term debt also being repaid. In the first half of fiscal 2026, working capital requirements increased due to planned inventory stocking and higher business activity in the aftermarket segment, but are expected to normalize to previous fiscal year levels by the end of the year. The company's liquidity position is also supported by a ca
sh surplus of Rs. 43 crore as of September 30, 2025, and available bank lines of Rs. 1,170 crore. As of October 2025, the company had utilized an average of 71% of its drawing power over a 7-month period, indicating a comfortable liquidity position.
Analytical Approach
Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of RML and its subsidiaries, Rane (Madras) International Holdings, B.V and Rane Auto Components Mexico S de R.L. de C.V. These entities are collectively referred to here
in as RML.
Please refer Annexure - List of Entities Consolidated, which captures the list of entities considered and their analytical treatment of consolidation.
Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Leading position in India’s auto steering market
RML is a leading player in the domestic steering market with strong presence in mechanical steering gears and hydrostatic gear systems. Further, RML has longstanding relationships with marque clients across vehicle segments, namely Maruti Suzuki India Ltd (MSIL; rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Ltd (TMPL; rated ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Tractors and Farm Equipment Ltd (TAFE; rated ‘Crisil AA+/Positive/Crisil A1+’) a
nd Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd (M&M; rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’). RML also acquired the steering component business of Yagachi Technologies in November 2021, for a consideration of Rs 23.19 crore. This acquisition has provided RML access to other major OEMs and has enhanced its leadership position in the domestic PV market.
Supported by its established presence, RML has managed to increase the share of business with its customers by bagging large orders. Besides, in-house capabilities have enabled t
he company to enhance products as per the requirement of key customers and sustain its healthy market position, despite competition from established peers such as ZF Steering Gear (India) Ltd and JTEKT India Ltd.
Diversified revenue profile post-merger completion
Post merger, RML has a diversified revenue profile with presence across market segments, namely domestic OEMs, aftermarket and exports. Domestic OEMs contribute 60-65% of revenue. Within OEMs, RML caters to PVs, CVs, and tractor segments.
The company also derives 15-20% of revenue from die-casting components sold to domestic and overseas customers. The company manufactures steering linkage products, engine valves, light metal castings, friction material an
d brake linings providing revenue diversity.
In fiscal 2024, RML had also floated a new subsidiary in Mexico, Rane Auto Components Mexico (RACM), which is likely to assemble some of its products from fiscal 2026, for supplies to customers in USA, Mexico and Canada. The company had invested $5 million till fiscal 2025 and is expected to invest another $3 million in RACM over the next few fiscals, however considering the current global scenario, RML has decided to act cautiously on its plans.
Crisil Ratings
expects RML to post healthy single-digit revenue growth over the medium term, supported by steady demand from OEMs, and better aftermarket sales, even as exports are likely to remain flattish over the medium term.
Improving financial risk profile
RML’s financial risk profile improved in fiscal 2025 post completion of the merger between RML and its group entities. Networth improved to Rs 663 crore as on March 31, 2025, and is expected to witness further improvement over the next 2 fiscals steadily supporte
d by increasing profitability and from one-time gains arising on the sale of non-core assets. Total debt on a combined basis as on March 31, 2025, was around Rs 761 crore as compared to Rs 701 crore a year earlier. Post completion of merger, gearing improved from 2.8 times as on March 31, 2024 to 1.15 times a year later and will improve further during the upcoming fiscals.
Besides, interest cover and debt/Ebitda are expected at over ~4.5 and at ~2.5 times, respectively, in fiscal 2026 as compared to 4.15
and 2.5 times, respectively, in fiscal 2025. Other debt metrics too will gradually improve over the medium term, supported by monetisation of non-core assets, which
is expected to be completed by fiscal 2027.
Earlier,
in September 2023, RML divested its step-down subsidiary, RLMC, which was incurring operational losses of Rs 40-50 crore per annum. Sluggish demand conditions, leading to delay in breakeven, would have resulted in sustained dependence on RML, which had already invested Rs 450-500 crore betwe
en fiscals 2016 and 2024. As on March 31, 2023, RLMC had total debt of $16 million, with $11 million of term debt and $5 million of working capital debt. In fiscal 2024, RML infused $16 million in RLMC to repay the debt and sold the entity to a US-based firm.
The sale consideration agreed with the buyer was around $4.9 million, as per acquisition terms and out of the same ~$2.9 million has been provisioned partly in fiscals 2024 and 2025.
Towards losses on investments in RLMC, an impairment of Rs 223 crore
was taken in fiscal 2023, while Rs 122 crore was recorded in fiscal 2024.
Established presence of Rane group
RML is part of the Chennai-based Rane group of companies, which has a consolidated turnover of around ~Rs 6,000 crore and manufactures a diverse range of automotive components, such as steering components, engine valves and brake components. Backed by a vintage of more than 80 years, the group has forged strong ties with leading OEMs in India and abroad.
Rane Holdings Ltd (RHL), the holding compa
ny of RML, infused equity of Rs 65 crore in fiscal 2018, Rs 15 crore in fiscal 2019, Rs 55 crore in fiscal 2021 and another Rs 30 crore in fiscal 2022, to support operations at RML, including part funding of capex. Supply of components, along with those of other group companies, to common customers, also helps RML rationalise freight cost.
Key Rating Drivers - We
a
knesses
Sizeable investments in the domestic die casting business in the past; modest but improving operating profitability
RML had made sizeable investments to expand its domestic die casting division during fiscals 2016 and 2017. However, slower-than-expected ramp up of facilities, due to volatility in end-user demand, led to net losses. The company has undertaken measures to tie-up businesses to enhance utilisation and there has been substantial improvement in the die casting division over the past two fiscals.
RML acquired Rane Light Metal Castings (RLMC) in the USA in 2016, but the subsidiary struggled with continuous losses, weighing on the company's returns. Initially, a 4-5 year turnaround period was anticipated, but RLMC continued to post significant net losses of over Rs 175 crore between fiscals 2017 and 2022. The losses were partly due to restructuring efforts, but weak demand from a major customer led to low revenue, perpetuating the losses. In fiscal 2023, RLMC recorded operating losses of over Rs 40
crore, prompting RML to divest its stake in the subsidiary in September 2023. The cumulative losses had a negative impact on RML's net worth. However, the sale of RLMC in fiscal 2023 resulted in a deferred tax benefit of Rs 113 crore, which is expected to reduce RML's tax liability over the next 2 fiscals, providing a positive offset to the earlier losses
.
RML's consolidated operating margin was previously constrained by the modest profitability of its light metal castings division, dropping to 3-5% in fi
scals 2020 and 2021. However, margins recovered to 8.4% in fiscal 2023, driven by improved standalone profitability. In fiscal 2024, margins declined to 7.4% due to weak sales in the CV segment and exports. The recent merger with group entities is expected to drive significant cost savings and efficiency gains, including unified procurement, logistics, and optimized support functions. These measures are anticipated to boost RML's operating margin to 9-10% over the next two fiscal years.
Besides, exports to
the US are moderate at ~10-12% of overall revenue, however there has not been a material impact on the business on account of the tariffs as most of its business to the US is on duty unpaid basis. RML remains cautious in its approach towards expansion of the business in the US and Mexico, especially in terms of making further investments.
Earlier, return on capital employed (RoCE) of RML declined to 8-10% between fiscals 2015 and 2017, from over 17% prior to fiscal 2013. RoCE gradually recovered to 13% in
fiscal 2018, but moderated in fiscals 2020 and 2021, owing to the expansion in the domestic die casting division. While it improved temporarily to over 10% in fiscal 2023, it fell again to 9% in fiscal 2024 on account of fall in operating metrics. However, with the merger between RML and its group entities, return metrics are expected to steadily improve over the medium term.
Exposure to demand cyclicality and pricing pressures from OEMs in the automobile industry
High dependence on OEMs renders the performance of RML partly vulnerable to the inherent cyclicality in the automobile industry and any prolonged slowdown particularly in the CV segment. However, revenue from aftermarket and exports provide respite, besides presence across sub-segments, which is expected to lend some stability to business. Raw material cost accounted for a substantial portion of revenue, while about 57% of the topline is derived from auto OEMs as of the first half of fiscal 2026 as compared
to two-thirds prior the merger between group entities in fiscal 2024.
Liquidity
Adequate
RML's liquidity profile remains robust, driven by annual cash accretion of Rs 200-220 crore from its operations, which comfortably covers its debt obligations of Rs 119 crore in the current fiscal and Rs 53 crore in the next. The company has also utilized proceeds from the sale of non-core assets (Rs 115 crore) to prepay long-term debt. With anticipated annual capex requirements of Rs 200 crore, the company plans
to fund these through internal accruals. Additionally, it will fund the construction of a commercial office building for Rane group companies (Rs 105 crore) through the sale of non-core assets. The company also has access to working capital limits of Rs 1170 crore, which were utilized at an average of 71% over the 7 months ended October 2025. Crisil Ratings notes that the company's liquidity is further supported by the possibility of equity infusion from its group, as seen in the past, when the holding com
pany provided support to the loss-making operations of RLMC.
Outlook
Stable
RML’s business risk profile is expected to improve driven by the continued domestic demand from the automotive sector and gradually enhance its operating profitability supported by operating leverage and synergy benefits due to the recently completed merger with its group companies. Better cash generation and prudent funding of capex, and proceeds from sale of non-core assets will be used to lower reliance on debt, improving the financial risk profile over the medium term
.

About the Company
RML is the flagship company of the Rane group, with the group holding company, RHL having 63.8% stake. Other group companies include ZF Rane Automotive India Pvt Ltd (joint venture) and Rane Steering Systems Pvt Ltd (wholly owned subsidiary of RHL, rated ‘Crisil A- / Stable’).
RML commenced manufacturing operations in 1960 and is one of the leading tier 1 automotive component suppliers engaged in the manufacturing of manual steering gears, hydrostatic steering systems, and steering and
suspension linkages,
which together account for about 80% of the overall revenue. The balance comes from its high-pressure aluminum die casting division.
The steerling linkage division and light metal casting division, erstwhile traditional RML business, has manufacturing units at Kanchipuram, Mysuru, Puducherry, Pantnagar and Hyderabad (2 units). The engine valve division (REVL) has manufacturing facilities at Ponneri and Tiruchirapalli (Tamil Nadu), Tumkuru (Karnataka), and Aziz Nagar and Medchal (Telangana), while the brake
lining business has plant locations in Chennai, Hyderabad, Puducherry and Tiruchirapalli.
