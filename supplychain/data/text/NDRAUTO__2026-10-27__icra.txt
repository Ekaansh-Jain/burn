Rationale
The ratings factor in the established presence of NDR Auto Components Limited (NDR Auto) in the automotive seating frame
and trims manufacturing industry, as well as the extensive experience of its promoters in the auto component industry. It
primarily caters to tier-I auto component suppliers such as Bharat Seats Limited (BSL; [ICRA]A+ (Stable)/A1), Toyota Boshoku
Automotive India Pvt. Ltd. (TBI) and Hyundai Transys India Pvt. Ltd., which in turn supply complete seating system to leading
original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) such as Maruti Suzuki India Limited (MSIL), Toyota Kirloskar Motor Pvt. Ltd.
(Toyota) and Kia Corporation (Kia), respectively. NDR Auto enjoys a healthy share of business (SoB) of around 34% for the
products its supplies to MSIL through BSL. The company’s revenues have grown at a strong compounded annual growth rate
(CAGR) of 37% during FY2022-FY2026, primarily on the back of healthy demand from MSIL and addition of other customers.
In FY2026, NDR Auto’s revenue grew by 15.4% to Rs. 822.5 crore, aided by improved demand with the Goods and Services
Tax (GST) reduction on passenger vehicles (PVs), ramp-up of new model wins and an increment in SoB with MSIL. It started
supplying seat trims to Kia models in July 2026 from its new plant in Anantapur, Andhra Pradesh. An increasing SoB with
OEMs, ramp-up in sales volumes for Kia models and the commencement of supply of new products such as seat insert fabric
and seat latch from January 2027 are likely to support 10-15% revenue growth in FY2027. It has entered technical
collaborations with foreign partners for seat latch systems, and electronic seat belt reminder systems, whose production is
likely to commence from Q4 FY2027. The company has a healthy order book of around Rs. 650 crore, across frame and trim
supplies and new product lines such as seat belt reminder, seat insert fabrics and seat latches to be executed by FY2030,
which provides healthy medium-term revenue visibility. The company’s operating profitability margin (OPM) was stable at
11% in FY2026; going forward, while margins in the core frames and trims business are expected to improve with operating
leverage, overall OPM is likely to remain range-bound due to product development costs and the gradual ramp-up of new
products.
The ratings continue to favourably factor in the company’s strong financial risk profile, characterised by healthy cash accruals,
low leverage and strong debt protection metrics. Its total debt/OPBITDA remained comfortable at 0.7 times as on March 31,
2026. NDR Auto invested Rs. 10 crore (as on March 2026) in a 50:50 joint venture (JV) with Hayashi Telempu, Japan, for
products such as ambient lighting, sunshades and other interior automotive components and is expected to further invest
Rs. 25-30 crore in this JV over FY2027 and FY2028 towards product development. It also plans to incur consolidated capex of

The ratings, however, remain constrained by the company’s high dependence on MSIL, given that around 70% of its revenues

automotive industry, with operations limited to the domestic market.
The Stable outlook reflects ICRA’s opinion that the company will likely record a healthy growth in revenues and earnings on
the back of new order wins and improving SoB with its key end-customer, MSIL, while maintaining healthy debt metrics.
Key rating drivers and their description
Credit strengths
Established relationships with customers and healthy SoB with MSIL provide revenue visibility – NDR Auto has an
established presence in the automotive frames and trims segment and enjoys long-standing relationships with leading
domestic OEMs and tier-I suppliers. The company supplies to MSIL and Toyota through BSL and TBI, respectively. It currently
caters to around 34% of MSIL’s frame and trim requirements across multiple models and has secured new orders for five new
models slated for launch over FY2027-FY2029, through BSL. These new model awards are expected to improve the company’s
SoB with MSIL over the medium term. For Toyota, NDR Auto supplies frames and trims for the Urban Cruiser and Hyryder
models and enjoys an estimated SoB of around 20%. The company also supplies trims for three KIA models and frames/trims
to Suzuki Motorcycle India Pvt. Ltd. (SMIPL) through BSL, in addition to supplying other metal components for the two-wheeler
(2W) segment. Increasing share of business with its established customer base and growth in content per vehicle are expected
to support the revenue growth.
Strong financial risk profile – The company’s capital structure remains comfortable, aided by low reliance on debt, as reflected
in gearing of 0.2 times and a total outside liabilities-to-net worth ratio of 0.6 times as on March 31, 2026. As of March 31,
2026, the company’s debt comprised only lease liabilities of Rs. 62 crore, which increased from Rs. 32 crore as on March 31,
2025 because of the leasehold land acquired for the company's greenfield projects under newly established subsidiaries. Its
interest coverage and TD/OPBITDA ratios remained robust at 27.7 times and 0.7 times, respectively, in FY2026. The company’s
debt protection metrics are likely to remain healthy, going forward, supported by healthy profitability and low debt levels,
with expected TD/OPBITDA of 0.5-0.6 times in FY2027.
Credit challenges
End-customer concentration risk with high dependence on MSIL – The company’s revenues are significantly dependent on
MSIL, as it derives around 70% of its top line from sales to the OEM (largely through BSL). Furthermore, its top two end-
customers, MSIL and Toyota, together accounted for 90-95% of its revenues in FY2026. This exposes the company to customer
and segment concentration risks. However, the risks are partially mitigated by MSIL’s market leadership in the PV industry and
the company’s healthy SoB with the OEM. Further, the company has added other OEMs, such as Kia, in FY2025. Going forward,
the company’s ability to onboard new customers remain crucial for deriving diversification benefits.
Exposed to inherent cyclicality in auto industry – NDR Auto caters to the automotive industry, which is highly sensitive to
economic cycles. Automotive OEMs were adversely affected over FY2020-FY2022 because of weak demand stemming from
Covid-19 challenges and semiconductor shortages, which impacted the performance of auto ancillaries. Hence, the
performance of the company remains vulnerable to the performance of the PV industry as well as to economic downturns.

Liquidity position: Strong
The company’s liquidity position is strong, characterised by expected retained cash flows of Rs 70-80 crore in FY2027, and
buffer of around Rs. 20 crore in working capital lines as of March 2026. Further, the company had cash and bank balances of
Rs. 35-40 crore as of March 2026. The company does not have any repayment obligations. It has capex plans of Rs. 50-60 crore
in FY2027 and is expected to invest Rs. 10-20 crore in the JV, to be funded through internal accruals. Moreover, the company
continues to enjoy healthy financial flexibility as part of the NDR Auto Group.
ESG related comments
Environmental considerations: Though NDR Auto is not directly exposed to climate-transition risks from the likelihood of
tightening emission control requirements, its automotive manufacturing customers remain highly exposed to the same.
Accordingly, the company’s prospects are linked to the ability of its end-customers (MSIL, Toyota, SMIPL, KIA) to meet
tightening emission requirements. Its exposure to litigation/ penalties from issues related to waste and water management
remains low.
Social considerations: NDR Auto, like most automotive component suppliers, has a high dependence on human capital.
Retaining human capital, maintaining healthy relationships with its employees and supplier ecosystem remains essential for
disruption free operations. Another social risk that the company faces pertains to product safety and quality, wherein instances
of product recalls and high warranty costs may lead to a financial implication and could affect the reputation and create a long-
lasting adverse impact. In this regard, its strong track record of catering to leading automotive OEMs underscores its ability to
mitigate these risks to an extent.
Rating sensitivities
Positive factors – The rating could be upgraded if the company demonstrates a sustained growth in its scale of operations,
driven by an uptick in its SoB with MSIL or diversification of its clientele, while maintaining healthy profitability and debt
metrics.
Negative factors – Weak demand in the PV industry or delays in commissioning and ramp-up of new product programmes,
resulting in reduced scale of operations and weak cash accruals that impact the company’s return indicators or debt metrics,
on a sustained basis, could result in a negative rating action. Large unanticipated debt-funded capex or investments exerting
pressure on liquidity or debt metrics, may also affect the ratings. Specific credit metrics that could lead to ratings downgrade
include Total Debt to OPBDITA above 1.5 times on a sustained basis.

About the company
NDR Auto Components Limited is a part of the NDR Auto Group, which traces its origins to the 1930s when its founder, Late
Shri N.D. Relan, began as a stockbroker in the financial services sector. In the 1980s, the Group diversified into auto component

manufacturing through BSL and Sharda Motor Industries Limited (SMIL). NDR Auto was incorporated in March 2019 as the
flagship company of the Group to demerge SMIL’s automobile seating business.
NDR Auto mainly manufactures frames and trims for the seating systems of PV and 2W OEMs through tier I suppliers. It caters
to various tier I suppliers, including BSL, TBI, Hyundai Transys, Bellsonica Auto Components Pvt. Ltd., etc. The company has six
plants, with one each at Gurgaon, Pathredi and Manesar (Haryana), Bangalore (Karnataka), Surendra Nagar (Gujarat) and
Anantapur (Andhra Pradesh). The company’s shares are listed on the s.
