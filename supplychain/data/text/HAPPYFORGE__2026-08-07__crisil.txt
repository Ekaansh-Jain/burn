Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil AA/Stable' rating on the long-term bank facilities of
Happy Forgings Limited
(HFL).
The rating action factors in the company’s healthy business risk profile, supported by successful diversification across end-user industries, moderate profitability, diversified clientele and strong financial risk profile. Revenue increased by ~9% in fiscal 2026 to Rs 1,546 crore driven by ~11% volume growt
h. Contribution from industrial and passenger vehicle (PV) segments increased to ~20% of revenue in fiscal 2026 (industrial: ~14%; PV: ~6%) from ~18% in fiscal 2024. The company is expanding to a value-added product portfolio, such as power generation, wind energy, railways, heavy engineering, data centre gensets, and oil and gas applications, thereby reducing its dependence on the cyclical automotive (auto) segment. Revenue is expected to grow by 10–15% over the medium term, supported by orders worth ~Rs 9
50 crore to be executed over the next three fiscals, out of which ~30% pertain to emerging segments with higher wallet share of the PV and industrial segments.
The company achieved healthy profitability of 30.4% in fiscal 2026, supported by improving machining contribution (~89% of revenue) and increasing contribution from high-margin crankshafts. The operating margin is expected to remain above ~29% over the medium term, supported by higher realisation from heavy forging and industrial segments and cost-r
ationalisation initiatives, including captive solar power generation.
HFL is undertaking capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 800–900 crore over fiscals 2027 and 2028 for a 125-tonne (T) hammer press and a captive solar project to support growth in emerging segments. Until the newly commissioned capacities achieve optimal utilisation, return on capital employed (RoCE) is expected to moderate to 16–17% over the near-to-medium term from ~21%.
While the capex is expected to increase debt by Rs 300 crore from
~Rs 330 crore as on March 31, 2026, the financial risk profile is likely to remain strong, supported by healthy cash accrual and capital structure. Debt protection metrics were comfortable, with interest coverage ratio of ~47 times in fiscal 2026 and expected to remain above 15 times over the medium term. The total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratio stood at 0.24 time and is expected to remain below 0.4 time over the medium term.
The rating continues to reflect the company’s diversified client
ele and longstanding relationships with marquee original equipment manufacturers (OEMs). Customer concentration risk remains low, with the largest customer contributing only ~13% of revenue and the top 10 customers accounting for ~60%.
These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to cyclicality in the end-user industries and exposure to risks related to execution and ramp-up of the ongoing capex.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Healthy
business risk profile supported by strong relationships with customers
The company has a track record of over four decades in the forged and precision-machined components business and maintains established relationships with leading dome
stic and global OEMs across commercial vehicles (CVs), farm equipment, PVs and industrial segments. The business risk profile has strengthened on the back of successful diversification into new end-user industries and products, reducing dependence on traditional auto segments. The company has increased its presence in industrial applications, such as power generation, railways, wind energy, oil and gas, heavy engineering and data centre gensets, and the industrial segment contributed to ~14% of revenue in f
iscal 2026, compared with ~2% in fiscal 2021. Contribution of the PV segment increased to ~6% in fiscal 2026 from ~1% in fiscal 2024, reflecting customer and product diversification.
Diversified segments
and customers
HFL has a diversified revenue profile, with established presence across domestic (~85% of revenue) and export markets (~15% of revenue) in fiscal 2026. The company caters to CV (~37% of revenue), farm equipment (~35%), off-highway (~8%), industrial (~14%) and PV (~6%) segments. Over the past
few years, it has significantly diversified its business risk profile, with the industrial segment contribution increasing to ~14% in fiscal 2026 from ~2% in fiscal 2021, supported by increasing presence in power generation, wind energy, railways, oil and gas, heavy engineering and data centre applications. The diversified end-user profile mitigates exposure to cyclicality in any single segment. Customer concentration risk is low, with the largest customer contributing to ~13% of revenue, supported by long
standing relationships with leading OEMs and tier-1 suppliers across segments.
The company benefits from its established position in the domestic forging industry, particularly in crankshafts and other complex forged and precision-machined components. It has strengthened its manufacturing capabilities by commissioning a 4,000 T press line and is undergoing expansion across forging and machining operations. Over fiscals 2027 and 2028, the company plans to undertake capex of Rs 800–900 crore towards the 125-
T hammer forging facility, machining expansion, solar power infrastructure and development of capabilities for heavy forged and machined components. The proposed expansion is likely to diversify the product and customer profiles while enhancing revenue visibility; however, timely execution of the capex without cost overrun remains monitorable.
Strong financial risk profile
The financial risk profile is likely to remain strong, supported by sizeable networth of ~Rs 2,109 crore as on March 31, 2026, healthy
cash accrual and moderate leverage despite the ongoing capex. Total debt stood at ~Rs 330 crore as on March 31, 2026, resulting in gearing of 0.16 time. Despite the planned capex, gearing is expected to remain below 0.3 time over the medium term. The debt protection metrics will remain comfortable, with interest coverage and net cash accrual to total debt ratios expected above 15 times and at 0.6–1.2 times in the medium term, supported by strong profitability.
Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses
Expo
sure to risks related to execution and ramp-up of the ongoing capex:
The company is undertaking sizeable capex over fiscals 2027 and 2028 towards 125-T hammer press, captive solar project and heavy forging. While the capex is expected to improve revenue and support diversification into higher value products over the medium term, the company is exposed to execution and ramp-up risks. Delay in project execution, slower-than-expected ramp-up of new capacities or lower-than-anticipated demand from industrial se
gments, such as data centres or heavy forging, will be monitorables. Until the newly commissioned capacities achieve optimal utilisation, RoCE is expected to moderate over the near to medium term to 16–17% from ~21%.
Susceptibility to cyclicality in CV and tractor segments:
The company remains exposed to cyclicality in key end-user industries such as CVs, farm equipment and off-highway equipment, wherein demand is dependent on economic activity, freight movement, infrastructure spending, agricultural out
put and farm incomes. This risk is mitigated by increasing diversification into industrial applications, PVs and export markets.
Liquidity
Strong
Net cash accrual is expected at Rs 375–520 crore per annum over fiscal 2027–2029 against limited term debt obligation. Average utilisation of fund-based working capital limit was modest at ~33% during the 12 months through June 2026. Strong cash accrual, bank limit and cash and equivalent of ~Rs 431 crore as on March 31, 2026, will adequately support th
e working capital requirement, capex and debt obligation over the medium term.
Outlook
Stable
HFL will continue to benefit from increasing revenue contribution of domestic and global customers, entry into new segments and growing contribution from industrial, data centre and export segments while sustaining strong financial risk profile despite the ongoing capex.

Kumar Garg (Chairman cum Managing Director) and Channan Ram Garg, supported by Ashish Garg,
Managing Director. The company manufactures forged and mach
ined components, primarily crankshafts, for the auto and non-auto segments. Its facility in Ludhiana has forging capacity of 1,52,000 TPA and machining capacity of 75,200 TPA.
