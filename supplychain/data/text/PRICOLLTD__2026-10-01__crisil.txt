Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has
continued its
rating on the long-term bank facilities
of Pricol Limited (Pricol) on
‘
Rating
Watch with Developing Implications’
.
The ratings of Pricol were placed on watch developing following their announcement at the stock exchanges on June 27, 2026, about the scheme of arrangement between Pricol and Pricol Autotech Limited (Pricol Autotech; a wholly-owned subsidiar
y of Pricol) and their shareholders and creditors. The scheme involves the demerger of the Driver Information and Connected Vehicle Solutions (DICVS) business to Pricol Autotech. The DICVS business contributed Rs 2,424.63 crore, representing 61.17% of Pricol’s consolidated revenue in fiscal 2026.
Post the demerger, the existing shareholders will receive one share of Pricol Autotech for every one share held in Pricol; hence, both entities will have mirror shareholding, with promoters holding ~38.5% stake in
both entities. The scheme is, inter alia, subject to receipt of statutory, regulatory and customary approvals, including approvals from the stock exchanges, National Company Law Tribunal, Chennai bench and shareholders and creditors. The process is expected to take 15–18 months to complete, and the effective date for the scheme will be April 1, 2027. The rating watch will be resolved upon completion of the demerger process and as clarity emerges on the profit and loss accounts and balance sheets of both th
e entities, and their business and capital expenditure (capex) plans. Weakening in the credit risk profile of Pricol owing to the demerger will remain a key rating sensitivity factor.
Pricol’s consolidated revenue rose to Rs 4,041 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 2,695 crore in fiscal 2025 driven by the addition of the injection moulded plastic component solutions (IMPCS) business, which was acquired by its subsidiary, Pricol Precision Products Private Limited (P3PL; ‘Crisil AA-/Watch Developing
’), in January 2025 from Sundaram Auto Components Limited (SACL, a subsidiary of TVS Motor Company Ltd).
Furthermore, the traditional business of Pricol grew ~22%, supported by two-wheeler (2W) volume growth of ~22.9%, change in the product mix driven by reforms in goods and service tax (GST) structure and healthy export. Over the medium term, revenue is expected to grow in healthy double-digits, supported by continued demand from original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), launch of new products, improvem
ent in the product mix and addition of new business.
The operating margin moderated to 11.6% in fiscal 2026 from 11.9% in fiscal 2025, impacted by modest increase in raw material prices and addition of low-margin business at the subsidiary. Nevertheless, after acquisition of the P3PL business by Pricol, its operating margin improved to 9.13% in fiscal 2026, supported by productivity enhancements, reduction in rejection rates and lower employee cost. P3PL is replacing some of its old machines and implementi
ng various cost optimisation measures, which are expected to support improvement in operating profitability.
Pricol achieved consolidated revenue from operations of Rs 1,105.44 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, representing 23.47% on-year growth, driven by strong automotive industry growth and new product introductions. The operating margins were at 11.25%, modestly higher than 11.05% achieved in previous corresponding period, driven by better utilization and operating leverage. However, the opera
ting margins were marginally lower on q-o-q basis on account of rise in input prices and rise in employee costs. Although the operating margin of the traditional business remains susceptible to changes in raw material prices despite having a passthrough clause with customers, it is expected to remain stable at 11–12% annually, supported by improvement in the product mix and continued cost optimisation measures.
The financial risk profile will remain healthy, driven by improving networth and moderate debt,
supported by prudent working capital management and capex. Debt, including acceptances, was Rs 363 crore as on March 31, 2026, compared with Rs 269 crore as on March 31, 2025. The company is planning capex of Rs 500 crore in fiscal 2027, which will be partly funded through healthy cash accrual of Rs 370–390 crore and the balance through debt.
Debt is expected to peak in fiscal 2027; however, gearing is expected below 0.5 time, while debt protection metrics, such as interest coverage and debt to earnings be
fore interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) ratios are expected at 10–11 times and ~1.5 times, respectively, over the medium term.
Besides annual cash generation, liquidity is supported by cash surplus of ~Rs 126 crore as on March 31, 2026, and adequate headroom in bank limit of Rs 105 crore, which was utilised over 46% for the 12 months through August 2026. Any inorganic growth opportunities, necessitating debt addition and impacting the debt protection metrics, will remain key rating sensi
tivity factors.
The rating continues to reflect the company’s established business risk profile, supported by healthy relationships with domestic OEMs, leading position in two-wheeler (2W) instrument clusters and improving revenue and product diversity owing to industry transition from mechanical clusters to light-emitting diode (LED) and thin-film transistor (TFT) clusters. The rating is also supported by benefits derived from the recent acquisition, improving operating efficiency and financial risk profi
le. These strengths are partially offset by revenue concentration in the 2W OEM segments and high dependence on imported raw materials, which render profitability vulnerable to foreign exchange (forex) rates.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Leading position in 2W instrument clusters and improving product and customer diversity, supported by healthy relationships with OEMs
Pricol has healthy market position in dashboard instruments and driver information systems with a tr
ack record of around five decades in supplying to leading domestic OEMs. On a standalone basis, the revenue share of dashboard instruments was ~71% in fiscal 2026 owing to improvement in the product mix, leading to better realisation. Pumps and mechanical products contributed 19% to the revenue in fiscal 2026, while switches and sensors accounted for the balance. Presence in different segments supports the product diversity. The company has in-house research and development (R&D) and track record of launchi
ng new products quickly. It plans to add new products to the portfolio, such as handlebars and battery management systems, which will enhance its product basket.
Globally, Pricol is the second-largest instrument cluster maker in terms of volume, next to Nippon Seiki & Co Ltd, Japan. Pricol also holds 55–60% market share in the domestic instrument cluster business and 65% market share in the 2W instrument clusters.
Majority of the revenue comes from domestic OEMs (~90% in fiscal 2026) with modest aftermar
ket and export presence. The 2W OEMs contribute ~70% to the revenue and the company has healthy relationships with key OEMs, such as TVS Motors, Hero MotoCorp Limited (‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Bajaj Auto Limited (‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Eicher Motors Limited (Royal Enfield 2Ws) and commercial vehicle (CV) OEMs - Tata Motors Limited (‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and Ashok Leyland Limited, and the construction equipment major, JCB India Limited (‘Crisil AAA/Stable’). The company earlier ha
d a non-compete agreement with its erstwhile joint venture partner, Denso Corporation, Japan, which recently expired, following which Pricol can supply to passenger vehicle (PV) OEMs.
Expected benefits from the acquisition of IMPCS division
The company’s segment diversification is set to significantly improve, with dependence on the dashboard segment (~75% share of revenue in fiscal 2025) reducing to ~54% in fiscal 2026 and sizeable contribution of over Rs 923 crore from the IMPCS business. The revenue of
P3PL is expected at more than Rs 1,000 crore in fiscal 2027, driven by new business. The operating profitability is expected to rise to 9.5–10.0% from 9.13% in fiscal 2026, driven by productivity enhancements, reduction in rejection rates and lower employee cost.
Sizeable portion of the products of Pricol and P3PL are electric vehicle (EV) agnostic; hence, the impact on revenue owing to a gradual shift towards EV will be negligible and revenue loss will be offset by new product launches over the medium t
erm.
Improving operating efficiency
Pricol has strong R&D capabilities and has started
manufacturing more critical components in-house, which ensures better quality and less wastage. Profitability was aided by the company’s focus on automation, which allowed for material pruning of the workforce, enhancing share of wallet with existing customers and moving up the value chain. Hiving off the loss-making overseas subsidiaries and change in the product mix in favour of better premium price complex products ha
s contributed to increase in operating margin from fiscal 2021. However, on account of moderate increase in raw material prices and acquisition of the low-margin IMPCS division in January 2025, the consolidated margin moderated to 11.85% in fiscal 2025 and to 11.62% in fiscal 2026. Nevertheless, P3PL’s operating margin
improved to 9.13% in fiscal 2026, driven by various cost optimisation measures, increased operating leverage, improved productivity through replacement of old machines and new business from O
EMs. Improvement in the operating margin at P3PL, along with healthy profitability from the existing business, is expected to keep Pricol’s consolidated operating margin steady at 11–12% over the medium term.
Operations were impacted by labour issues and strikes in the past. Any major labour issue impacting operations will be monitorable.
Healthy financial risk profile
Strong cash accrual since fiscal 2021 and rights issue of Rs 81 crore in fiscal 2021 enabled debt reduction, strengthening the balance she
et and debt protection metrics. Gearing was 0.32 time as on March 31, 2026, compared with 0.22 time as on March 31, 2025, as the company availed debt of Rs 127 crore to fund the acquisition of the IMPCS division and increase working capital debt to Rs 236 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 114 crore as on March 31, 2025. The company incurred capex of ~Rs 323 crore in fiscal 2026, which was partly funded through debt, leading to increase in total debt to Rs 363 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 269 crore
as on March 31, 2025. In fiscal 2027, capex of ~Rs 500 crore will be undertaken, which will be partly funded through debt. The debt is expected at Rs 550–600 crore but is expected to decrease with repayment. Gearing is expected to peak at 0.4–0.5 time as on March 31, 2027, and improve over the medium term, while debt to Ebitda ratio is expected to remain below 1.3 times. Nevertheless, the debt protection metrics will be healthy, as reflected in interest coverage and net cash accrual to total debt ratios of
above 10 times and 0.5–0.7 time, respectively, over the medium term.
Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses
Revenue dependence on the 2W segment
Domestic OEMs contribute 89–90% to the revenue, with export contributing ~5% while the aftermarket segment makes up the balance. The company has a small share in the aftermarket segment due to long lifecycle of products. Similarly, export revenue share is minimal on account of strong competition from global giants. Large exposure to domestic OEMs makes Pricol vulnera
ble to the auto demand cycle and offtake by customers. Pricol has improved its segmental revenue with 2Ws contributing ~70%. However, prolonged slowdown in 2W demand, as seen during fiscals 2020–2022, impacts offtake for components.
Revenue dependence on the 2W segment may continue, although Pricol has taken initiatives to improve its share in the PV segment (8–9% of sales in fiscal 2026), where it is already in discussions with some OEMs. Besides, improved demand from CVs will help reduce revenue depende
nce on the 2W segment. However, given its strong relationships with 2W OEMs and leading position in the instrument cluster business, revenue concentration on OEMs and the 2W segment will persist over the medium term.
High import dependence rendering profitability vulnerable to adverse forex movements
The company imports 45–50% of its raw materials (LCD and TFT screens), as against ~40% earlier, driven by increased imports in the TFT segment. Of this, 65–70% comes from China and the remaining is sourced fr
om South Korea, Japan and Taiwan. All imports are routed through its subsidiary in Singapore, Pricol Asia Pte Limited. Large import and limited hedging expose profitability to fluctuations in forex and freight costs, especially during volatile global markets.
Liquidity
Strong
Liquidity will be supported by healthy cash accrual of Rs 370–390 crore over the medium term and cash balance of ~Rs 126 crore as on March 31, 2026. While the cash accrual will be adequate for meeting the debt obligation of Rs 25–30
crore and Rs 40–50 crore in fiscals 2027 and 2028, respectively, and the working capital requirement, Pricol is expected to avail debt of Rs 200–250 crore for capex of Rs 500 crore. The company has access to bank limit of Rs 130 crore (utilised ~46% on average during the 12 months through August 2026).

About the Company
Pricol commenced operations in 1975 in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, and is one of India's leading dashboard manufacturers. The company offers driver information systems and sensors, pumps and allied products, telema
tics and wiping systems catering to leading auto OEMs in 2W and 3W, PV, CV, farm equipment and offroad vehicle segments across India and overseas (45 countries) with more than 2,000 products. The company had 15 manufacturing plants (including subsidiaries) across India (Coimbatore, Manesar, Pantnagar, Pune, Sricity, Mysuru, Hosur,
Narasapura, Chennai, Nalagarh, Bhiwadi and Sanand), one manufacturing unit in Indonesia, and three international offices (Dubai, Tokyo and Singapore).
On June 30, 2026, the promo
ters, Vikram Mohan and his family members, held 38.5% stake in Pricol, foreign portfolio investors held 13.95%, mutual funds held 9.86%, alternate investment funds held 1.89%, and public and others held the balance.
