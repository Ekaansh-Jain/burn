Rationale
Infomerics has reaffirmed the long-term and short-term ratings of the bank facilities of
Autoline Industries Limited (AIL). The reaffirmation of ratings factors in AIL’s established
relationships with leading domestic automotive OEMs, including Tata Motors, Mahindra,
Ashok Leyland, Daimler India and Cummins, supported by repeat orders and healthy
fresh business inflows, providing strong medium-term revenue visibility.
The ratings also draw comfort from the healthy 25.2% growth in operating income to
₹822.29 crore in FY2026, successful commercialisation of multiple new vehicle
programmes, integrated manufacturing and engineering capabilities, adequate available
capacity with capacity utilisation of around 61.8%, and strengthening of the capital
structure, with tangible net worth improving to ₹192.17 crore and gearing declining to
1.65x as on March 31, 2026 from 2.46x a year earlier. However, the ratings continue to
be constrained by high customer concentration, with Tata Motors Limited and Tata
Motors Passenger Vehicles Limited contributing around 65% of revenues. The ratings
also factor-in the moderate debt protection metrics with Total Debt/EBITDA of 4.06x and
interest coverage of 1.96x for FY26. Other limiting factors for the rating are elevated
working capital intensity, susceptibility of margins to steel price movements and
programme stabilisation costs, as well as exposure to cyclicality in the automotive
industry.

Key Rating Drivers with Detailed Description
Strengths
Established relationships with leading OEMs coupled with healthy order inflows
provide strong revenue visibility
AIL has developed long-standing relationships with leading domestic automotive OEMs
including Tata Motors Limited (TML), Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Limited (TMPVL),
Mahindra Vehicle Manufacturers, Ashok Leyland, Daimler India and Cummins. The
company continues to maintain a preferred Tier-I supplier status with OEMs for several
structural sheet metal, Body-in-White (BIW), mechanical assemblies and driver control
systems across commercial vehicles (CVs), passenger vehicles (PVs) and electric vehicle
(EV) platforms. During FY2026, Tata Motors and Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles
together contributed around ~65% of the company's revenues, reflecting strong
customer acceptance as well as repeat business from existing platforms.
The relationship with key OEMs strengthened further during FY2026 with successful
commercialisation of several new vehicle programmes including Tata Sierra, Nexon
Facelift, Punch Facelift, Altroz Facelift, Mahindra Udo three-wheeler platform, all of which
commenced production during the year. Management indicated that these programmes
were implemented largely as per schedule, with only minor delays, and have already
started contributing to revenues. The company has also secured approximately ₹600

crore of fresh business from existing customers, providing healthy revenue visibility over
the medium term. The continued addition of new platforms across both passenger and
commercial vehicle segments reduces dependence on individual vehicle models while
strengthening customer stickiness.
Healthy improvement in operating scale supported by successful execution of
expansion projects and new product launches
AIL reported a healthy improvement in its scale of operations during FY2026, with
operating income increasing by 25.2% to ₹822.29 crore from ₹656.93 crore in FY2025.
The growth was driven by higher production volumes from recently commercialised
vehicle platforms, increasing share of passenger vehicle programmes and higher tooling
revenues. According to the management, the company benefited from improved
production schedules across Tata Motors and Mahindra during the year, supported by
favourable demand in passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles and EV platforms. The
management further highlighted that the company's quarterly revenues increased
steadily during FY2026 as new programmes stabilised. Management also stated that the
current growth is not dependent on a single platform but is being driven by a combination
of existing products, new model launches and increasing volumes from previously
launched programmes, thereby improving the sustainability of future revenues.
Integrated manufacturing capabilities and strong engineering competencies
enhance competitive positioning
AIL has developed integrated manufacturing capabilities covering the complete value
chain of sheet metal stamping, robotic welding, mechanical assemblies, Body-in-White
(BIW) assemblies and tooling, enabling the company to manufacture more than 4,000
different automotive components for multiple OEM platforms. The company operates
press capacities ranging from 63 tonnes to 2,000 tonnes and has increasingly adopted
robotic welding cells to improve manufacturing precision, consistency, productivity and
quality.
The company is also supported by its in-house engineering capabilities through Autoline
Design Software Limited (ADSL), which undertakes product design, engineering and
software services for automotive applications. During FY2026, the Board approved the
amalgamation of ADSL with Autoline Industries, which is expected to improve
operational integration, eliminate duplication of corporate overheads and strengthen the

company's ability to offer integrated design-to-manufacturing solutions to OEM
customers.
Improvement in capital structure supported by equity infusion, asset monetisation
and strengthening of net worth
The company's financial profile improved materially during FY2026 following successful
equity infusion and completion of the monetisation of its investment in Autoline
Industrial Park recognised in FY26(A) at Rs. 21.84 Cr. Tangible net worth increased
significantly to ₹192.17 crore as on March 31, 2026, from ₹116.46 crore a year earlier,
supported by the addition of PAT and fresh equity infusion during FY26 through the
conversion of 22 lakhs preferential warrants into equity shares. The company allotted 22
lakh equity shares at an issue price of ₹102.50 per share (face value of ₹10 and securities
premium of ₹92.50), resulting in a total equity infusion of approximately ₹22.55 crore.
The company has another potential source of equity infusion on the allotted 32.65 lakh
warrants at an issue price of ₹75 per warrant (face value ₹10 and premium ₹65) on which
it has already received 25% of the issue price upfront. Despite an increase in absolute
borrowings to fund business expansion and tooling requirements, the company's overall
gearing improved to 1.65 times as on March 31, 2026 from 2.46 times as on March 31,
2025. Management further indicated that a significant portion of the tooling-related term
loans are expected to be repaid through customer receivables (for billing done for client
specific tools developed) during FY2027, which is likely to support further deleveraging
going forward.
Growing contribution from tooling business and focus on productivity
enhancement support future profitability
Apart from its core automotive component business, AIL has strengthened its tooling
operations, which have become an increasingly important value-added business
segment. Management indicated that tooling revenues increased to approximately ₹75
crore during FY2026, substantially higher than the earlier expectation of around ₹40
crore, supported primarily by higher orders from Tata Motors. The company has also
initiated measures to enhance its in-house tool room capabilities and reduce dependence
on outsourced tooling. Going forward, management intends to focus largely on
automation, robotic welding, productivity improvements and cycle-time optimisation
rather than undertaking significant capacity expansion. These initiatives are expected to

improve manufacturing efficiencies, reduce dependence on skilled labour and support
gradual improvement in operating margins over the medium term.
Adequate available manufacturing capacity provides headroom for future growth
with limited incremental capex
The company operated at a capacity utilisation of around 61.8% during FY26, broadly in
line with the previous year, despite reporting a 25% increase in operating income. This
was primarily driven by higher value addition per unit through increased supplies of
complex assemblies, and commencement of supplies for new OEM platforms, rather than
a significant increase in production volumes. Further, ramp-up of the recently
commissioned facilities at Pune, Sanad and execution of higher-value orders from key
customers such as Tata Motors and Mahindra supported revenue growth without
materially impacting overall capacity utilisation. This indicates the availability of
sufficient installed manufacturing capacity to support expected growth over the near to
medium term without requiring significant greenfield investments. Management
indicated that future capital expenditure would largely be directed towards automation,
balancing existing production lines, installation of additional robotic welding cells and
productivity enhancement rather than major capacity additions. Accordingly, a
significant portion of the anticipated revenue growth over FY2027-FY2029 is expected
to be generated through better utilisation of existing manufacturing infrastructure, which
is likely to moderate future capital intensity
Weaknesses
High customer concentration despite diversification across multiple vehicle
platforms
Although AIL supplies components across passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles and
EV platforms, its customer concentration remains relatively high. During FY2026, Tata
Motors Limited and Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Limited together accounted for
nearly 65% of total revenues, while the top three customers contributed over 70% of
total sales. While the relationship with Tata Motors spans multiple vehicle platforms and
component categories, thereby reducing dependence on any single model, the company's
revenues remain exposed to changes in production schedules, procurement policies,

inventory rationalisation and demand conditions affecting a limited number of OEMs.
Consequently, customer concentration continues to remain a key credit monitorable
despite continued order additions.
Operating margins remain susceptible to raw material price movements and
programme stabilisation risks
Steel continues to constitute the principal raw material for the company, accounting for
nearly 65–68% of total revenue, exposing profitability to fluctuations in steel prices.
Although the company benefits from periodic price revisions with OEMs, there could be
timing differences in pass-through of raw material cost increases, particularly during
periods of sharp commodity price volatility. Further, despite healthy revenue growth
during FY2026, EBITDA margin moderated to 9.47% from 10.30% in FY2025.
Management attributed the decline primarily to costs associated with stabilisation of
newly launched programmes such as Sierra, Nexon Facelift and Mahindra Udo, which
required manpower training, production balancing and lower initial operating
efficiencies. Although these programmes have now largely stabilised and management
expects margins to improve during FY2027, profitability continues to remain exposed to
commodity prices and execution efficiencies.
Working capital intensity continues to remain relatively high, constraining
liquidity
The company's operations remain inherently working capital intensive owing to
inventory requirements, tooling execution and customer credit terms. During FY2026,
the operating cycle increased to around 68 days from 59 days in the previous year, while
inventory holding increased to approximately 43 days in FY26 from 37 days in FY25. Cash
flow from operations also moderated sharply to ₹11.23 crore during FY2026 from ₹67.27
crore in FY2025. Management clarified that the deterioration was largely attributable to
approximately ₹75 crore of tooling inventory that had been manufactured and kept ready
for dispatch towards the end of FY2026 but was billed only during FY2027. While
management expects normalisation of working capital during FY2027, liquidity remains
sensitive to execution of tooling orders, inventory management and timely customer
collections.

Debt protection metrics continue to remain moderate despite improvement in the
capital structure
Despite the strengthening of the company's net worth during FY2026, debt protection
metrics continue to remain moderate owing to expansion-related borrowings and higher
working capital requirements. Total debt increased to ₹316.55 crore as on March 31,
2026 from ₹286.28 crore a year earlier, while interest coverage moderated to 1.96 times
from 2.13 times in FY2025. Similarly, Total Debt/EBITDA remained elevated at 4.06
times. Nevertheless, the company's credit profile remains dependent on timely
improvement in debt protection metrics through sustained profitability and
deleveraging.
Exposure to cyclicality in the automotive industry and concentration in domestic
OEM production schedules
AIL derives virtually its entire revenue from the automotive sector and therefore remains
exposed to cyclical fluctuations in vehicle demand, regulatory changes, consumer
sentiment and OEM production schedules. Although the company has diversified across
passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, EVs, tooling and engineering services, its
revenues continue to remain closely linked to domestic automotive production. While
management expects sustained growth supported by infrastructure spending, uptick in
commercial vehicle sales volumes, increasing EV penetration and implementation of the
vehicle scrappage policy, any prolonged slowdown in the automotive sector or reduction
in production volumes by key OEMs could adversely impact the company's operating
performance.
Liquidity – Adequate
The company's liquidity is expected to remain adequate, supported by healthy expected
cash accruals, improving debt protection metrics and moderation in leverage. The overall
gearing improved to 1.69x as on March 31, 2026 from 2.57x as on March 31, 2025, aided
by equity infusion, conversion of CCDs into equity and asset monetisation. Additionally, a
significant portion of the tooling-related term loan is expected to be repaid through
milestone-based receipts from Tata Motors during FY27, which should support debt
servicing. However, liquidity remains constrained by the high utilisation of fund-based

Rating Sensitivities
Upward Factors
- Sustained growth in scale of operations along with improvement in operating
margins and cash accruals.
- Improvement in the capital structure and debt protection metrics, with overall
gearing reducing below 1.0x and interest coverage improving above 3.5x on a
sustained basis.
- Sustained improvement in liquidity through stronger cash flow generation and
efficient working capital management
Downward Factors
- Material decline in operating income and/or profitability, resulting in weakening of
debt protection metrics.
- Deterioration in the financial risk profile, with overall gearing remaining above 2.0x
and/or interest coverage declining below 2.0x on a sustained basis.
- Elongation in the working capital cycle or adverse raw material price movements,
resulting in pressure on liquidity and incremental debt requirements.
About the Company
Autoline Industries Ltd (incorporated on December 16, 1996, as Autoline Stampings
Private Ltd.) was initially set up in January 1995 as a partnership firm known as "Autoline
Pressings". The registered office of the company is located at Nanekarwadi, Chakan, Pune.
The company is engaged in the manufacturing of auto components (sheet metal) and is a
supplier to Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) and automobile companies. The
company has three business divisions, namely Medium and Large Stamped Assemblies,
Mechanical Assembly/Driver Control Systems and Concept, Styling, Designing, Analysis,
and Application Software Services
