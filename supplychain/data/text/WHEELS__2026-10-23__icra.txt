Rationale
The upgrade in the ratings of Wheels India Limited (WIL/the company) factors in the company’s healthy operating performance
and the recently concluded equity fund raise, which have cumulatively improved its credit metrics. This improvement is
expected to sustain going forward, despite the advancement of sizeable capex plans. The company is one of the largest steel
wheel manufacturers and has an established market position, with a diversified presence across segments, customers and
products. Given its established market position, steady underlying industry demand, and expansion into and ramp-up of new
products, the company reported healthy revenue growth of 15.5% in FY2026 and 17.8% in Q1 FY2027. This momentum is likely
to sustain over the medium term. Additionally, the company raised Rs. 430 crore through a qualified institutional placement
(QIP)/private placement in September 2026, the proceeds of which are being utilised towards debt reduction. While the
company is advancing its capex plans in FY2027 to around Rs. 450 crore to enhance capacity for future needs, its debt metrics
are expected to improve. Total Debt/OPBITDA is expected to reduce to less than 2.5 times over the medium term from 3.1
times in FY2026, supported by the fund raise and steady internal accruals.
The ratings also factor in the company’s established relationships with automotive and construction equipment original
equipment manufacturers (OEMs) in India and globally. It enjoys a dominant domestic market share in commercial vehicle
(CV), tractor and passenger vehicle (PV) steel rims. Further, the company is among the larger manufacturers of air suspension
products used in buses and trucks. It has a well-diversified revenue profile, with 24% of revenues derived from overseas
markets. Its presence across segments further strengthens this diversification, with 55% of revenues derived from the auto
segment and the remaining 45% from non-auto segments such as fabrications, windmill components, earthmover components
and hydraulics, among others.
The ratings also remain supported by WIL’s exceptional financial flexibility arising from its parentage. The company belongs to
the T S Santhanam faction (TSF) of the larger TVS Group of Companies, an established name in the domestic auto ancillary
industry. The T S Santhanam group held a 56.86% stake in the company as on September 30, 2026, through Trichur Sundaram
Santhanam & Family Private Limited (26.20%, rated [ICRA]AA+ (Stable)/[ICRA]A1+), TSF Investments Limited (26.57%) and India
Motor Parts and Accessories Limited (IMPAL) (4.09%, rated [ICRA]AA (Stable)/[ICRA]A1+).

The ratings also factor in the susceptibility of the company’s earnings to forex fluctuations and geopolitical developments,
given its dependence on export markets for 25% of revenues. Its earnings are also exposed to the inherent cyclicality of the
domestic automotive industry, which accounts for a significant portion of the remaining revenues. Nevertheless, the
company’s established customer relationships, healthy order book, addition of new customers, diversification into non-auto
businesses and increased share of business with existing customers as part of vendor diversification initiatives by global OEMs
mitigate these risks to an extent. These factors are likely to support its medium-term revenue growth prospects.
The Stable outlook on the long-term rating reflects ICRA’s expectation that the company will be able to sustain its credit profile,
supported by its healthy business profile and cash accruals.
Key rating drivers and their description
Credit strengths
Financial flexibility as a part of the T S Santhanam Group (TSF) – WIL enjoys exceptional financial flexibility and lender/investor
comfort, given its association with the T S Santhanam Group, a faction of the larger TVS Group and an established name in the
domestic auto ancillary industry. The TSF group holds a 56.86% stake in the company through Trichur Sundaram Santhanam
& Family Private Limited (26.20%), TSF Investments Limited (26.57%) and IMPAL (4.09%). Mr. Srivats Ram, a fourth-generation
TVS family member, is the Chairman and Managing Director and oversees the company’s day-to-day operations.
Established position as one of the largest steel wheel manufacturers in India – With an operating income of Rs. 5,482.4 crore
in FY2026 and over five decades of presence in the steel wheel industry, the company is among the largest manufacturers of
automotive steel wheels in India and earthmover steel wheels globally. It has established relationships with automotive and
construction equipment OEMs in India and overseas. During FY2026, WIL commanded a dominant domestic market share at
the consolidated level, with shares of 46% in commercial vehicles (CV), 52% in tractors and 34% in passenger vehicle (PV) steel
rims. Further, the company is among the larger manufacturers of air suspension products used in buses and trucks and holds
a healthy market share with domestic OEMs in this segment.
Diversified segment and customer base; non-auto segment constitutes around 45% of revenues – The company derived
around 76% of its consolidated revenues from India in FY2026, with the remaining 24% from overseas markets. In terms of
segments, around 55% of revenues was derived from the auto segment and 45% from non-auto segments such as fabrications,
windmill components, earthmover components and hydraulics, among others. The company also has a diversified customer
base that includes large players such as Caterpillar Inc., Tata Motors Limited (TML), Ashok Leyland Limited (ALL), Mahindra and
Mahindra Limited (M&M) and Maruti Suzuki India Limited (MSIL). This diversification mitigates the risks arising from a revenue
decline in any single customer or segment to an extent.
Credit challenges
Moderate operating profit margins, albeit improvement in FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 – Operating margins remain moderate at
7.8% in FY2026 and 7.5% in Q1 FY2027, despite a significant improvement from the 4-5% levels recorded in FY2023 and FY2024.
This improvement was aided by price corrections, a higher proportion of margin-accretive products and cost optimisation
measures, including procurement and production efficiencies and fixed-cost controls, among others. The company’s operating
profits are expected to improve going forward, supported by periodic price negotiations with customers, a continued focus on
cost optimisation, a favourable product mix, an increasing proportion of exports and better operating leverage. The extent
and sustainability of this improvement remain critical for a meaningful improvement in credit metrics.
Revenues exposed to demand dynamics in overseas markets, forex and commodity price fluctuations, and domestic auto
industry cyclicality – WIL derived around 24% of its revenues from export markets in FY2026, exposing it to potential demand
slowdowns in overseas markets and forex fluctuations. The company predominantly supplies the earthmover, windmill
component, passenger vehicle and tractor segments in export markets. Its revenues are also exposed to the inherent cyclicality
of the domestic automotive industry, while its earnings remain dependent on its ability to pass on commodity price fluctuations
to customers. Nevertheless, the company’s established customer relationships, healthy order book, addition of new customers

and increased share of business with existing customers as part of vendor diversification initiatives by global OEMs mitigate
revenue risks to an extent. These factors are likely to support healthy medium-term revenue prospects.
Ongoing sizeable capex; however, debt metrics are expected to be supported by fund raise and internal accruals – The
company has advanced its capex plans in FY2027 to Rs. 450 crore to enhance capacity for future needs. However, it raised Rs.
430 crore through a QIP/private placement in September 2026, with the proceeds being utilised towards debt reduction.

than 2.5 times over the medium term from 3.1 times in FY2026. This improvement is expected to be supported by the fund
raise and steady internal accruals.
Environmental and social risks
Environmental considerations – The company's business profile remains susceptible to changes in OEM preferences,
particularly the increasing shift towards lightweighting, which is driving higher penetration of alloy wheels and could affect
demand for WIL’s products, which are primarily steel-based. Consequently, WIL's ability to enhance its alloy wheel offerings
and improve its product mix remains important for sustaining its market position. The company is also exposed to increasingly
stringent environmental regulations relating to waste management and pollution control, which could result in higher
operating costs and increased capital expenditure for new capacity installations.
Social considerations – Social considerations for WIL primarily relate to maintaining healthy industrial relations and ensuring
product safety. Attracting and nurturing skilled manpower is critical as the company seeks to keep pace with innovation and
technological advancements. On the product front, vehicle recalls by OEMs due to defective auto components could result in
additional costs and liabilities.
Liquidity position: Adequate
WIL’s liquidity remains adequate, supported by anticipated cash flow from operations of Rs. 250-300 crore per annum and
undrawn working capital lines of around Rs. 170.0 crore as on June 30, 2026. The company had a cash balance of Rs. 25.5 crore
as on June 30, 2026. Additionally, it raised Rs. 430 crore through a QIP/private placement in September 2026. Against these
cash sources, the company has sizeable capex plans of Rs. 450 crore in FY2027 and around Rs. 200-250 crore p.a. in FY2028
and FY2029, which are likely to be funded through a combination of internal accruals and debt. Further, the company has
scheduled debt repayments on its existing loans at the consolidated level of Rs. 43.0 crore in FY2027, Rs. 43.6 crore in FY2028
and Rs. 37.8 crore in FY2029. Overall, ICRA expects WIL to meet its near-term commitments through internal cash generation
while maintaining a modest liquidity cushion.
Rating sensitivities
Positive factors – Strengthening of the company’s earnings profile with meaningful improvement in operating profit margins
and return indicators, along with a improvement in financial leverage, could lead to a rating upgrade. Specific quantitative
trigger for an upgrade includes Total debt/OPBITDA less than 1.5 times on a sustained basis.
Negative factors – Pressure on WIL’s rating could arise from a sustained weakening in its revenues or earnings, significant debt
funded capex, delay in ramping up of new capacities, leading to a deterioration in liquidity or leverage metrics and return
indicators.

About the company
Wheels India Limited (WIL) is among the largest manufacturers of steel wheels in India and operates across the automotive
(except two-wheelers), tractor and earthmover segments. The company is part of Trichur Sundaram Santhanam & Family
Private Limited, a faction of the TVS Group. In addition, WIL manufactures air suspension systems for luxury buses and multi-
axle trucks in India and supplies fabricated and machined parts for windmills. The company derives around 75% of its
consolidated revenues from the domestic market and operates manufacturing facilities across 12 locations. Through its
subsidiary, WIL Car Wheels Limited (WCWL, rated [ICRA]A (Stable)/[ICRA]A1), it manufactures passenger car steel wheels; WIL
holds a 74% stake in this entity, while the remaining stake is held by Topy Industries Limited, Japan.
