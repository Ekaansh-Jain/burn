Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1' ratings on the bank facilities of Hero Motors Limited (HML).
The ratings continue to reflect HML’s healthy business risk profile, supported by a diverse mix of value-added components and strong customer profile, supporting profitability and revenue diversification. Furthermore, the rating also reflects the
expected improvement in HML’s financial risk profile with reduction of debt from the proceeds of recently concluded initial public offering (IPO), resulting in comfortable debt protection metrics.
These strengths are partially offset by investment towards subsidiaries in the ramp-up phase, customer concentration in the revenue profile and exposure to cyclicality in the automotive (auto) components industry.
HML reported consolidated revenue of Rs 1,191 crore in fiscal 2026, registering 9% on-year growth. T
he growth was driven by a 19% increase in the powertrain segment, supported by rising contributions from e-mobility products and a diversified domestic and international customer base. Revenue growth in the alloy and metals (A&M) segment remained moderate as the company continued to increase its focus on the higher-growth powertrain business. Consequently, the revenue mix shifted in favour of powertrain, which contributed 54% of revenue in fiscal 2026 compared with 49% in fiscal 2025. Revenue is expected to
grow steadily over the medium term, supported by mid-teen growth in the powertrain segment and steady performance of the A&M business. The powertrain segment’s share in the overall revenue mix is expected to increase further over the medium term.
Consolidated adjusted operating margin (excluding non-cash share-based expenses) improved to 13.0% in fiscal 2026 from 10.8% in fiscal 2025. The improvement was driven by favourable foreign exchange movements, given HML’s export exposure, and operating leverage b
enefits. Profitability is expected to marginally moderate in fiscal 2027 due to elevated commodity, freight and logistics costs arising from ongoing geopolitical uncertainties, which will be passed on to customers, albeit with a lag. Thereafter, operating margin is expected to recover and stabilise at 12.5–13.0% from fiscal 2028 onwards, supported by recovery of costs, and higher operating leverage.
HML’s financial risk profile is adequate and improving. The company had consolidated debt of Rs 504 crore, i
ncluding vendor financing of Rs 103 crore, against adjusted networth of Rs 419 (reported net worth of Rs 480 crore) crore as on March 31, 2026. Adjusted gearing remained stable at 1.2 times for the last two fiscals, while interest coverage ratio improved to 3.9 times in fiscal 2026, from 3.6 times in fiscal 2025. HML successfully completed its IPO in September 2026. The promoter shareholding decreased from 85.57% (pre-IPO) to 61.63% (post-IPO) following the Rs 1,000 crore IPO, which included Rs 400 crore of
fer for sale by the promoters and the balance Rs 600 crore being infused into HML. Networth is expected to cross Rs 1,000 crore this fiscal. Debt is likely to be reduced by Rs 190 crore, Rs 200 crore will be utilised for planned capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 300–400 crore over the next three to four fiscals, and the remaining Rs 210 crore is being retained for general corporate purpose and inorganic growth opportunities. Debt protection metrics are expected to improve over the medium term, with gearing
likely to settle below 0.5 time, while interest coverage ratio will exceed 5 times.
Liquidity is expected to remain strong, supported by annual net cash accrual of Rs 100–150 crore over the medium term. Unutilised IPO proceeds, after debt reduction and funding capex requirements, will be invested in liquid instruments and utilised for general corporate purposes and future inorganic growth opportunities. The company also has fund-based bank limits of Rs 273 crore, with utilisation at around 62% in past 12
months as of July 2026.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Healthy business r
isk profile, driven by increasing
revenue diversification:
HML has progressively increased its focus on high value-added and technologically complex components, resulting in a more diversified product portfolio and improved operating performance over the years. The company has also continued to diversify beyond its traditional alloy and metals business into higher-margin segments, supporting its overall profitability profile.
Geographical diversification has strengthened considerably, with exports contribu
ting ~41% to revenue in fiscal 2026 compared with a negligible share in fiscal 2018. Revenue increased to Rs 1,191 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 904 crore in fiscal 2022, reflecting healthy business expansion across segments. Growth over the medium term is expected to be supported by new product additions, scale-up of subsidiaries and increasing contribution from the powertrain business. The company also expects a significant rise in revenue from the electric mobility segment over the medium term.
HML’s bus
iness risk profile is expected to remain healthy, supported by growth in high value-added segments, expanding customer relationships, and continued product diversification, leading to improved revenue diversity. However, the pace of growth in the powertrain segment and the successful ramp-up of new businesses and subsidiaries will remain monitorable.
Adequate financial risk profile despite high capex intensity
:
HML’s financial risk profile is adequate and improving. The company had consolidated debt of Rs
504 crore, including vendor financing of Rs 103 crore, against adjusted networth of Rs 419 (reported net worth of Rs 480 crore) crore as on March 31, 2026. Adjusted gearing remained stable at 1.2 times for the last two fiscals, while interest coverage ratio improved to 3.9 times in fiscal 2026, from 3.6 times in fiscal 2025. Networth is expected to cross Rs 1,000 crore with receipt of IPO proceeds of Rs 600 crore. Debt is also likely to be reduced by Rs 190 crore, while Rs 200 crore for planned capex of Rs
300–400 crore over the next three to four fiscals, and the remaining Rs 210 crore is being retained for general corporate purpose and inorganic growth opportunities. Debt protection metrics are expected to improve over the medium term, with gearing likely to settle below 0.5 time, while the interest coverage ratio will exceed 5 times. Any further support to the group entities or sizeable debt-funded acquisitions will remain monitorable.
Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses
Investment towards subsidiaries
in ramp-up phase:
The company has expanded its capabilities and product offerings through strategic investments and acquisitions. It acquired a stake in Hewland Engineering Limited (HEL) to strengthen its presence in the high-performance two-wheeler and four-wheeler segments. It has also established subsidiaries such as HYM Systems Private Limited and Hero EDU Systems Private Limited to support expansion into new geographies and product categories. Additionally, HML acquired 100% ownership of Spur in Novem
ber 2023 from Hero Cycles Limited (HCL) to cater to both existing and new customers. While contribution from the subsidiaries to the group's operating performance was modest until fiscal 2025, HEL reported improved profitability in fiscals 2024 and 2025, and is estimated to have maintained stable performance in fiscal 2026.
Several subsidiaries remain in the scale-up phase and are expected to receive continued financial support for capex and growth initiatives in the near term. Their successful operationa
l ramp-up and transition towards sustainable profitability are expected to support the group's overall performance over the medium term and will remain key monitorable factors.
Customer concentration in revenue and exposure to cyclicality in the auto components industry
:
HML’s business is derived from original equipment manufacturers (OEMs). Demand in the sector is inherently cyclical and influenced by several external factors, including consumer disposable income, rural and urban consumption trends, credi
t availability, fuel prices, regulatory changes and overall economic conditions. Consequently, any slowdown in industry volume or adverse macroeconomic developments could affect order flow and growth prospects. Although the company has made steady progress in diversifying its customer base across geographies and product segments, customer concentration remains moderate, with the top five customers accounting for 60–65% of revenue.
While longstanding relationships with leading OEMs provide revenue visibili
ty and business stability, dependence on a limited set of customers exposes the company to risks arising from changes in sourcing strategies, loss of market share by key customers, production disruptions, or adverse demand conditions in the end-user markets. Sustained diversification of the customer base, with increasing contribution from newer products, overseas markets and high value-added segments, will remain critical to mitigating customer concentration risks and supporting HML’s business risk profile.
Liquidity
Strong
Unencumbered cash and liquid investments stood at Rs 25 crore as on March 31, 2026. Liquidity is expected to remain strong, supported by healthy annual net cash accrual of Rs 100–150 crore over the medium term. Furthermore, the company's liquidity profile is expected to strengthen significantly following the IPO, with surplus proceeds, after debt reduction and funding growth capex requirements, being deployed in liquid investments and available to support future inorganic growth
opportunities. HML also has access to fund-based bank limits of Rs 273 crore, with utilisation at around 62% in past 12 months as of July 2026. Overall, liquidity is expected to remain adequate to support debt servicing, capex requirements and strategic growth initiatives.
Outlook
Stable
The business risk profile of HML will remain healthy over the medium term, with addition of customers and products, and ramp-up of subsidiaries. The financial risk profile is also expected to witnes
s sustained improvement with steady cash accrual and prudent capex spend, resulting in comfortable debt protection metrics.
