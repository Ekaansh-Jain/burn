Detailed Rationale
CRISIL Ratings has reaffirmed its 'CRISIL A+/Stable/CRISIL A1' ratings on the bank loan facilities of Hero Motors Ltd (HML). CRISIL Ratings has
withdrawn
its rating
on the non-convertible debentures (NCD) of Rs 33 crore at the request of the company and on receipt of the requisite
documentation. This is in line with the CRISIL Ratings policy on withdrawal of rating on debt instruments.
The ratings continue to reflect the healthy business risk profile of HML, driven by improving contribu
tion from high value-added components leading to higher margin and increasing revenue diversification over the years. However, the o
perating income remained flat in fiscal 2024, with consolidated revenue of Rs 1,066 crore owing to contraction in exports due to slowdown in the European markets. Despite the flat revenue, the adjusted operating margin (after adjusting non-cash share-based expense amounting to Rs 39.5 crore) increased to 10.2% in fiscal 2024 from 9.9% in fiscal 2023, driven by improvement in th
e gross margin that was partially offset by an increase in the employee benefit expenses on account of hiring new personnel for building capacities and expansions. The subsidiaries, which were loss making in fiscal 2023, have contributed positively to the overall profitability in fiscal 2024, driven by significant ramp-up in Hewland Engineering Ltd. Other subsidiaries are also expected to scale up in the near term and will remain monitorable. The operating performance is expected to improve gradually with p
ick-up in demand and launch of new products over the medium term and will remain monitorable. Increased revenue contribution from the high-margin segments should lead to gradual margin expansion over the medium term.
The ratings also factor in the strong financial risk profile with consolidated debt of Rs 304 crore as on March 31, 2024. To fund growth, the company is expected to undertake capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 400-500 crore over the next 3-4 fiscals through a mix of debt and internal accrual.
CRISIL Ratings has taken note of the company withdrawing its draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) on October 5, 2024. The company filed the DRHP in August 2024, for a proposed initial public offering (IPO) of Rs 900 crore, including fresh issue of shares worth up to
Rs 500 crore and offer for sale (OFS) of
Rs 400 crore by promoters—OP Munjal Holdings, Bhagyoday Investments and Hero Cycles Ltd. The company is in the process of evaluating the re-filing of DRHP in the near future.
The ratings continue to re
flect the healthy business
risk profile of HML, driven by improving revenue diversification and strong financial risk profile despite the sizeable capex. These strengths are partially offset by investment towards subsidiaries in the ramp-up phase, customer concentration in the revenue profile and exposure to cyclicality in the automotive (auto) components industry.
Analytical Approach
CRISIL Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of HML, its subsidiaries and joint ventures. This is because all these entities have common management and strong business and financial linkages.
CRISIL Ratings has
included the debt
of
Spur Technologies Pvt Ltd
(Spur; rated ‘CRISIL A+(CE)/Stable/CRISIL/BBB+/Stable’), which has been guaranteed
by HML,
to arrive at
the
adjusted
debt.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description
Strengths
:
Healthy business risk profile, driven by increasing
revenue diversification:
HML has gradually included high value-added components and has built capabilities in more complex parts, leading to significant improvement in the product diversity and operating performance in the recent years. The company continues to diversify its operations from the alloy and metals business to other business lines that fetch incremental margin.
Geographical diversity has improved, with exports contributing to around 40% of the revenue in fiscal 2024, compared with a negligible share in fiscal 2018. The overall revenue rose significantly to Rs 1,055 crore in fiscal 2023 from Rs 904 crore in fiscal 2022, however, it remained nearly flat in fiscal 2024 at Rs 1,066 crore.
While revenue growth has been flat in fiscal 2024, the recovery is expected over the medium term driven by product addition and ramp-up in subsidiaries. Furthermore, the company expects the contribution from the electric vehicles business to increase significantly over the medium term. The business risk profile may remain healthy, driven by growth in the high value-added segments and the addition of customers and products, leading to improving revenue diversity.
Strong financial risk profile despite high capex intensity
:
Consolidated debt stood at Rs 304 crore against adjusted net worth of Rs 346 crore as on March 31, 2024. The company is expected to undertake significant capex of Rs 400-500 crore in the next 3-4 fiscals that will be funded through a mix of debt and equity. The debt protection metrics remained healthy in fiscal 2024, as reflected in the estimated interest coverage ratio of 3.9 times. Interest coverage ratio is expected to remain over 3.5 times over the medium term.
Weaknesses
:
Investment towards subsidiaries in ramp-up phase:
The
company
has
acquired stake in Hewland Engineering Ltd to gain capabilities in the high-performance two-wheeler and four-wheeler segments. Similarly, it has set up other subsidiaries recently,
such as HYM Systems Pvt Ltd and Hero EDU Systems Pvt Ltd,
for expansion into new geographies and product lines. HML also acquired 100% stake in Spur in November 2023 from Hero Cycles Ltd, to cater to existing and new customers of HML. Until fiscal 2023,
the subsidiaries had negligible contribution to the operating
performance of the company. While Hewland Engineering Ltd witnessed improvement in its operating profitability during fiscal 2024, other subsidiaries are yet to contribute to the overall operating profitability.
Hence, these subsidiaries will require financial support
for funding their capex in the near term. Ramp-up in operations of the subsidiaries and their contribution to the overall operating performance over the medium term will be monitorable.
Customer concentration in revenue and exposure to cyclicality in the auto components industry
:
Revenue is largely dependent on demand from original equipment manufacturers in the two-wheeler industry, which in turn depends on several factors, such as disposable incomes, consumer preferences, credit availability and fuel prices. Furthermore, while the company has gradually added customers, the top five customers contributed to around 65% of the revenue in fiscal 2024. This dependence poses a risk to operating performance in case of any significant slowdown.
Liquidity
: Strong
Cash and liquid investments stood at Rs 99 crore as on March 31, 2024. Fund-based limit was utilised at 75-80% during the six months through September 2024. Healthy annual cash accrual, expected at Rs 75-100 crore from fiscal 2025, will sufficiently cover moderate debt obligation and working capital requirement. The company has significant capex of Rs 400-500 crore planned over the next 3-4 fiscals, to be funded through mix of debt and internal cash accrual.
Outlook
:
Stable
The business risk profile of HML will remain healthy over the medium term, with addition of customers and products, and ramp-up of subsidiaries. The financial risk profile may continue to be strong despite significant capex of Rs 400-500 crore planned over the next 3-4 fiscals, to be funded through a mix of debt and internal cash accrual.
Rating
s
ensitivity factors
Up
ward factors:
Continued high revenue growth and operating margin sustaining over 14%
Addition of customers and products, leading to revenue diversification and significant improvement in cash accrual
Sustained improvement in cash accrual or any equity infusion
Down
ward factors:
Lower-than-expected revenue growth or delayed ramp-up in subsidiaries, with operating margin sustaining below 10%
Stretch in the working capital cycle
Large, debt-funded capex or acquisition
About the Company
Incorporated in 1998, HML is a part of the OP Munjal group, whose flagship company is Hero Cycles Ltd (rated ‘
CRISIL A+/Negative/CRISIL A1’)
. HML manufactures and supplies auto components including gearboxes, continuous variable transmission and other components for the two-wheeler and electric bicycles segments.
The company demerged its auto components business into Hero Cycles Ltd with effect from April 1, 2014.
The auto components division of Hero Cycles Ltd was merged back into HML i
n December 2022 with effect from
April 1, 2022
.
